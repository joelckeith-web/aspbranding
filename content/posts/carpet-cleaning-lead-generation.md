Carpet cleaning lead generation is the work of bringing in new residential and commercial customers and then bringing them back for the next cleaning. For a company doing $500,000 to $600,000 a year with several crews, it runs through Local Services Ads, search pages and a current Google Business Profile. It also runs through Facebook and Instagram, and through follow-up built on your job records.

This guide covers each of those pieces, plus how to keep new-customer jobs and repeat jobs apart in your reports. It draws on guidance from the Carpet and Rug Institute, Google's help pages and Census data, along with how we approach [carpet cleaning marketing](/industries/carpet-cleaning) at ASP.

## How Carpet Cleaning Demand Repeats

The Carpet and Rug Institute (CRI) recommends having carpet [professionally cleaned every 12 to 18 months](https://carpet-rug.org/carpet-for-homes/cleaning-and-maintenance/). It gives the same interval for [carpet in businesses](https://carpet-rug.org/carpet-for-business/cleaning-and-maintenance/). That turns every finished job into a future reminder date. By CRI's guidance, a customer you clean for this spring is due again 12 to 18 months later.

Commercial work comes with its own standards. CRI publishes CRI 204, a commercial carpet maintenance and cleaning standard for business facilities. The [ANSI/IICRC S100 standard](https://iicrc.org/s100/) sets procedures for professional cleaning of commercial and residential carpet and rugs. Its 2021 seventh edition is current, and a revision is under way. Naming the standard your crews follow gives a facility buyer something concrete to compare.

Most companies in the trade are small. In 2023, [Census County Business Patterns](https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/cbp/about.html) counted 6,673 carpet and upholstery cleaning establishments with paid employees. Of those, 4,829 had fewer than five employees, 256 had 20 to 49, and 47 had 50 to 99. A company running several crews has more to show a buyer: a wider service area, commercial capacity, and certified technicians if your team holds the credential.

**Key Takeaway:** CRI's 12-to-18-month guidance gives every job a natural reminder date. A multi-crew company also has wider coverage, commercial capacity and credentials worth putting in front of buyers.

## Local Services Ads for Carpet Cleaners

Carpet cleaning services is one of Google's [Local Services Ads categories](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/6224841?hl=en&co=GENIE.CountryCode%3DUS) in the US, in areas where Google runs it. Google screens each business before its ads go live, and you pay per lead rather than per click.

Placement comes from an auction. Google explains this on its page on [how Local Services ads are ranked](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/7527305?hl=en). The auction looks at your bid, how likely the ad is to bring in a lead, and your profile quality. Google lists your rating, number of reviews, average response time, high-quality photos and completed verification checks as parts of profile quality.

Reviews and response time are the two your team affects every day. With several crews finishing jobs, a review request after every job keeps the profile growing. Keep those requests inside Google's review policies and the Federal Trade Commission's rule on fake reviews and testimonials. That means no fake reviews and no free or discounted services in exchange for a review. For response time, someone in the office has to own every new lead, and our guide to [speed to lead](/blog/speed-to-lead-home-service-businesses) explains why the first reply matters.

If you run more than one location, plan coverage first. Google notes that when one business has multiple locations serving the same area, Local Services shows only the highest-ranking ad for a search.

**Key Takeaway:** Local Services Ads reward a growing review count and fast replies. Build the review request into your follow-up, and build the lead hand-off into how your office works.

## Google Search Ads, Facebook and Instagram

Google Search Ads reach the homeowner who has a fresh stain, a pet accident or a move-out date coming up. That buyer has a problem right now, so the ad should lead to a page about that exact service, with your phone number easy to tap.

Facebook and Instagram work differently. They fit a low-ticket recurring service well. A seasonal offer can sell directly to past customers and to homes on streets where your crews already work. Your own customer list can serve as the audience for those offers.

We run Google, Local Services and Meta campaigns through [PPC management](/ppc) and [Meta ads management](/meta-ads), and we judge each one on booked jobs, not clicks.

**Key Takeaway:** Use search ads for the customer who needs a cleaning now, and social ads for past customers and nearby homes that will need one again.

## Trust Signals Carpet Cleaning Buyers Check

CRI's [guide to hiring a carpet cleaning professional](https://carpet-rug.org/testing/seal-of-approval-service-providers/hire-a-carpet-cleaning-professional/) gives buyers a set of questions to ask. Two of them are whether the technicians hold IICRC certification and how long the company has been in business. Put both answers where buyers will see them: on your website, your Business Profile and your ads.

Manufacturer warranties give buyers another reason to care. CRI says carpet manufacturers recommend, and sometimes require, Seal of Approval certified cleaners for warranty protection. Cleaners who use Seal of Approval solutions with Seal of Approval equipment can be recognized as Seal of Approval service providers. That recognition is worth showing on your site.

IICRC also offers a credential for the company itself, the IICRC Certified Firm, next to its certification for technicians. If your company holds either one, show it.

**Key Takeaway:** Show your IICRC certification, years in business and any Seal of Approval recognition, because CRI's guidance points buyers to those signals.

## Repeat Customers and Booked-Job Reporting

In a recurring business, the first job and the repeat jobs need separate credit. The first job goes to the channel that brought the customer in. Every repeat cleaning after that is retention, and it gets credited to your follow-up, not to the original ad. If you mix the two, your ads look better than they are, and customers who stopped coming back disappear from view.
