Most marketing audits are sales pitches with a spreadsheet attached. You book a call, sit through sixty minutes of flattery, and land on a slide that says "buy more of what we sell." A real home service marketing audit is supposed to work the other way. It should be built to sometimes tell you not to buy anything at all. That's the posture behind the $99 audit ASP runs for roofers, HVAC companies, plumbers, electricians, and remodelers. Last week it meant telling a company to walk away from the exact service they called us about. Here's why that's the whole point, and what an honest audit digs into.

## What Makes a Home Service Marketing Audit Honest

Most contractors have been burned by a marketing pitch dressed up as a diagnosis. The "audit" finds five problems, and all five happen to be things the agency conveniently sells.

An honest audit has to be able to land anywhere, including nowhere. If the true answer after sixty minutes is "keep doing what you're doing," that goes in the deck. If the answer is "fire your agency and fix these two settings yourself," that goes in the deck too. No upsell required.

That posture costs money in the short term. It would be easier to sell every business the same lead generation package and call it a day. But being straight with a contractor is worth more over five years than a retainer they resent is worth over five months. Contractors talk to other contractors. Word gets around fast in this industry, faster than most marketers want to admit.

**Key Takeaway:** An audit that never says "don't buy this" isn't an audit. It's a pitch wearing a disguise.

## The Lead Generation Trap We See Constantly

Last week a company came to us asking about lead generation. That's a common enough request. What happened next isn't.

After walking through their buyer, their service area, and how their past jobs closed, the honest answer was clear: for their specific buyer, that channel doesn't exist. Buying it, from ASP or from anyone else, would have burned money on a search that never happens the way they imagined. What they needed instead cost a fraction of what they were braced to spend.

The proposal we sent them included a slide listing everything we were deliberately not selling them. That slide probably cost us short-term revenue. It also told them exactly what kind of company they were working with going forward.

This kind of mismatch is common in home service marketing. A business assumes the fix is more leads, when the real gap is closing the leads they already get, or showing up correctly when a homeowner searches by name. Our post on [lead generation beyond word of mouth](/blog/beyond-word-of-mouth-lead-generation) breaks down why that assumption trips up so many contractors before they've spent a dollar.

**Key Takeaway:** More leads isn't always the fix. Sometimes the real leak is upstream, in how your business shows up or how fast you respond, not in how many new names hit your CRM.

## What the 60-Minute Audit Covers

The audit runs through five areas, in this order:

- **Ads.** Whether current ad spend is reaching the right buyer, and whether that spend is even being tracked correctly. Small setup mistakes here quietly drain budgets, a problem we cover in [common PPC mistakes small businesses make](/blog/avoid-these-5-common-ppc-mistakes-small-businesses-make).
- **Local and organic search.** How the business ranks when someone nearby searches for the service, and whether the basics of [local SEO for home service businesses](/blog/local-seo-for-home-service-businesses) are in place. This matters more than most owners realize, because a homeowner searching for a service near them is usually ready to book, not browsing.
- **Answer engines and virtual assistants.** How search tools and voice assistants describe the business when someone asks for a recommendation, a newer piece of visibility most audits skip entirely.
- **The website's conversion path.** Whether the site turns clicks into booked calls, which often matters more than which platform gets more traffic. We dig into that trade-off in [Google Business Profile vs. website priority](/blog/gbp-vs-website-home-service-priority).
- **CRM and attribution.** Whether the business can trace a dollar of ad spend all the way to a signed job, or whether that connection breaks somewhere in the middle.

**Key Takeaway:** A real audit doesn't stop at traffic and rankings. It follows the money all the way to a signed job, or it flags exactly where that trail goes cold.

## Why Marketing Spend Without Attribution Is a Guess

The CRM check might be the least glamorous part of the audit and the most valuable. A lot of home service businesses can tell you how many leads came in last month. Far fewer can tell you which channel produced the jobs that got signed, scheduled, and paid.

That gap matters because budgets get set based on gut feeling instead of results. The [U.S. Small Business Administration](https://www.sba.gov/blog/how-get-most-your-marketing-budget) points to research putting average small business marketing spend near 8% of revenue, but that benchmark only means something if you know which piece of that spend is producing work orders. Without a clean line from spend to signed job, a business is budgeting in the dark.

This is where a lot of otherwise well-run contractor businesses lose money quietly. Our breakdown of [marketing attribution for home service businesses](/blog/marketing-attribution-home-service-businesses) walks through what a working CRM setup should look like, and how to tell if yours already tracks this correctly.

**Key Takeaway:** If you can't trace a signed job back to the channel that produced it, you're not managing a marketing budget. You're guessing with real dollars.

## What You Walk Away With: The 10-Slide Growth Deck

At the end of the sixty minutes, every business gets the same format: a 10-slide deck ranking the top three to five moves by impact. Not a laundry list of forty ideas. Not a generic template. A short, specific list built from what showed up in the ads account, the search rankings, the website, and the CRM during the call.

Sometimes that list includes hiring ASP for a piece of it. Sometimes it doesn't. The deck is built the same way either way, because the goal is a clear next step, not a signed contract.

## Why Straight Answers Beat Retainers You Resent

Here's the math behind the posture. A retainer sold on inflated promises might close faster. It also tends to get canceled inside six months, once the results don't match the pitch. A contractor who gets a straight answer, even a disappointing one, tends to come back when the timing is right, and tells other contractors about it in the meantime.

That's the bet: honesty pays out over years. A padded pitch pays out over months, if it pays out at all. For a business built on repeat calls and referrals, that's not even a close call.

**Key Takeaway:** A marketing partner who tells you "not yet" or "not this" is playing a longer game than one who tells you "yes" to everything you ask for.
