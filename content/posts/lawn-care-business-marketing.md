Lawn care business marketing works best when it follows the growing season and treats each kind of work as its own product. A weekly mowing customer, a retaining wall customer and a snow removal customer buy different jobs on different calendars. Each one needs its own pages, ads and offers. Every lead should be tracked to a booked job.

It is a big, local trade. The Bureau of Labor Statistics counted 123,133 private landscaping services establishments in the US in 2024, with average employment of 900,726. This guide covers how to market a landscaping business so it stands out in the neighborhoods it serves. It uses what Google and federal sources have published, plus how ASP runs [landscaping marketing](/industries/landscaping) for lawn, landscape and snow removal companies.

## Seasonal Demand

Landscaping runs on the growing season. The Bureau of Labor Statistics says [some grounds maintenance jobs are seasonal](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/building-and-grounds-cleaning/grounds-maintenance-workers.htm). It adds that workers may be busier or work longer hours in spring, summer and fall. Employment points the same way: US private employment in landscaping services was 740,983 in January 2024 and 995,164 in July 2024, about 34% higher in July.

That figure counts workers, not customer demand, and it covers one year. It still shows how far the trade's workforce swings, and it is a good reason to plan marketing a season ahead. Spring service pages and seasonal content should be live before the season starts, not halfway through it. Ad budgets should lead into your busy months and pull back when your crews are full.

Snow removal adds a winter calendar for companies that sell it. If you plow, shift weight to winter services as the mowing season winds down. Once tracking has run for a season, set the calendar from your own booked-job history instead of a guess.

**Key Takeaway:** Plan pages and ad spend one season ahead. Once you have a season of your own booked-job data, use it instead of national patterns.

## Maintenance and Design-Build Work

The federal [definition of landscaping services](https://www.census.gov/naics/resources/model/dataHandler.php?input=561730&chart=2022&search=Y) runs from lawn and landscape maintenance to installing trees, shrubs, plants, lawns and gardens. It also covers designing and building walkways, retaining walls, decks, fences and ponds. Those are different jobs with different buyers. One catch-all services page or one ad cannot speak to all of them well.

Recurring maintenance is a lower-ticket, repeat purchase, so it can carry a seasonal offer directly. Design and hardscape work is a longer decision. Those buyers need project photos, detailed service pages and an offer like a design consultation or an estimate.

Customer value should shape the budget. A recurring maintenance customer can be worth a full season of visits, which changes what you can afford to pay to win the first one. Work back from what a booked customer is worth, not from a flat monthly number.

**Key Takeaway:** Market recurring maintenance and design-build work as two separate products, each with its own offer and its own budget math.

## Google Business Profile and the Map Pack

Google's [help page on local ranking](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091?hl=en) names relevance, distance and prominence as the main factors in local results. It also says there is no way to request or pay for a better local ranking. Prominence depends partly on how many websites link to your business and how many reviews you have. That means your profile has to match the services you sell, cover the area you serve and keep earning reviews.

Google's [Business Profile guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177?hl=en) also limit how you set up profiles. A business that travels to customers and has no storefront gets one service-area profile. That area shouldn't stretch farther than about 2 hours of driving from where you're based. Google does say a larger area can suit some businesses. Separate profiles are allowed only for locations with their own service areas and their own staff.

Send a review request with a direct Google review link as part of your follow-up after each job. Stay inside Google's review guidelines and the FTC's rule on fake reviews. Keep the request clean, with nothing offered in exchange, no quotas for crews and no suggested wording.

**Key Takeaway:** One accurate service-area profile with steady, honest reviews is worth more than a stack of extra profiles that break Google's rules.

## Service Pages and SEO

Your website carries the searches homeowners make before they are ready to call. Build pages in order of what a booked job is worth to you, not in order of search volume. A service with steady demand, good margin and weak pages from the companies already ranking gets built first.

Over time a page grows into a cluster: the main service page plus the cost breakdown, the method comparison and the seasonal problem, all linked together. Sourced detail makes each page more useful. An irrigation page, for example, can cite [EPA WaterSense](https://www.epa.gov/watersense/outdoors). WaterSense says residential outdoor water use in the US is nearly 8 billion gallons a day, mainly for landscape irrigation.

The most useful details come from you or a senior crew lead, such as what a typical job costs and why a cheap install fails. Credentials belong on the page too. The National Association of Landscape Professionals offers a [Landscape Industry Certified Technician](https://www.landscapeprofessionals.org/certification) certification for experienced technicians in softscape or hardscape installation, maintenance and irrigation.

Fix the technical side before adding pages. Pages Google can't index, redirect chains and slow mobile pages come before cosmetic fixes. New content goes in on a schedule over months rather than all at once. Our [landscaping SEO](/seo/landscaping) page lays out how that work is scoped and reported.

**Key Takeaway:** Build pages for your most valuable services first and fill them with details only your crews know.

## Local Services Ads and Google Search Ads

We checked Google's [US Local Services Ads category list](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/6224841?hl=en) in October 2026. It included Landscaping services, Lawn care services, Tree services and Snow removal as separate categories. That gives each kind of work a category to apply under, with pay-per-lead ads in the placement above the map results. Google screens every business before ads run.

Your position inside Local Services Ads comes from an auction that weighs your bid and your profile quality. Google's [help page on ad rankings](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/7527305?hl=en) says rating, number of reviews, average response time, images and completed verification checks feed profile quality. It also says missed calls may hurt responsiveness. Our piece on [speed to lead](/blog/speed-to-lead-home-service-businesses) covers why answering fast matters.

Google Search Ads are where install jobs get targeted by name. Give each service or service area its own ad group with phrase match, so you can tell which service is paying. Grow the negative keyword list every week.

Watch the geography too. Service-area businesses routinely pay for clicks outside the drive time they would accept, so compare where the spend went with where your crews want to work. Our [landscaping advertising](/ppc/landscaping) page shows how we set up these accounts.

**Key Takeaway:** Apply for the Local Services Ads categories that match your work, keep reviews and response times strong, and use Search Ads to go after installs by name.

## Facebook and Instagram Ads

Facebook and Instagram don't catch someone searching for a mowing service today. They keep your name in front of homeowners in your area between searches and bring back people who read your site and didn't call. Turn on retargeting before any money goes to cold audiences, because past site visitors are the cheapest audience you have.

Use your own work as the creative. Photos and video of projects your crews built carry design and hardscape ads, while stock images of somebody else's job are a common reason trade accounts underperform. A service-area audience also needs a steady supply of new creative, which our [Meta ads](/meta-ads) page covers.

Meta helps with hiring too, since the crew members you want are usually working and not browsing job boards. Grounds maintenance workers held about 1.3 million jobs in the US in 2025. BLS projects employment in the occupation to grow 5 percent from 2025 to 2035. Run recruiting as its own campaign with its own budget so hiring spend is never read as lead-generation performance.

**Key Takeaway:** Start Meta with retargeting and real project photos, and keep recruiting in its own campaign.

## Tracking Booked Jobs and Repeat Customers

Clicks and calls are the start of the report, not the end. Call tracking tags each phone lead by source, forms carry their source into your CRM, and that source stays on the job record where your CRM supports it. Fix tracking before changing bids, because an ad account optimizes toward whatever it counts.

Recurring customers need one more rule. Credit the first job to the channel that won the customer, and count every repeat visit after that as retention, not new acquisition. Mixing the two would let a season of mowing revenue inflate the numbers for whichever ad brought the customer in. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do you market a landscaping business?

Start with a complete Google Business Profile and steady review requests, then build a service page for each kind of work you want more of. Add Google Search Ads and, if you pass Google's screening, Local Services Ads. Use Facebook and Instagram for retargeting, seasonal offers and project photos, and track every lead to a booked job.

### Should lawn care and design-build work be marketed the same way?

No. Recurring maintenance is a lower-ticket, repeat purchase, so it can carry a seasonal offer directly. Design and hardscape work is a longer decision, so it needs project photos, detailed service pages and an offer like a design consultation or an estimate. Many companies sell both, so plan each side on its own.

### Can landscaping companies run Google Local Services Ads?

Yes. When we checked in October 2026, Google's US category list included Landscaping services, Lawn care services, Tree services and Snow removal. Google screens each business before its ads run. Once you are live, your bid, your reviews and your response time all feed where your ads show.

### Do I need a separate Business Profile for every town I serve?

No. Google allows a service-area business without a storefront one Business Profile. You can have more than one only if you have separate locations with their own service areas and staff. Coverage of individual towns comes from your service area settings and your website instead of extra profiles.

### How should a landscaping company handle the slow months?

Plan around them instead of running the same budget all year. Spend and content lead into your busy season and pull back when crews are full. Companies that sell snow removal shift weight to winter services. After a season of tracked jobs, your own numbers set the calendar.

### Should snow removal get its own page?

If you sell it, yes. Snow removal is a different service with a different season and a different buyer decision than lawn care, and one combined page serves neither well. Build the page before winter so it is indexed and ready when the first storm hits.

### How do I track which landscaping marketing is working?

Call tracking tags phone leads by source, forms carry their source into your CRM, and the source stays on the job where your CRM supports it. Count repeat visits as retention so mowing revenue does not inflate the channel that won the customer.
