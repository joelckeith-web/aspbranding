If you fired your marketing agency tomorrow, would you keep your website, your domain, your ad accounts, your leads, and your data? For a lot of home service operators, the honest answer is "I'm not sure" — and that uncertainty is the whole problem. This post explains what owning your marketing data actually means, the seven questions that reveal whether you own yours, and the contract red flags that tell you you're about to hand control to someone else.

Here's the uncomfortable version. Some agencies build your marketing on accounts they control — the website on their hosting, the Google Ads account under their umbrella, the analytics in their name. As long as you're a happy, paying client, you never notice. The day you try to leave, you find out the website goes dark, the leads stop, and the ad history you paid years to build disappears. The leverage was never about the work. It was about who held the keys.

## The horror story every operator should hear once

We've watched this play out more than once. An operator gets frustrated with their agency, gives notice, and goes to switch. Then the calls start. The new provider asks for access to the website — and it turns out the old agency owns the hosting and the domain, and won't release them without a fight or a fee. The Google Ads account? Built under the agency's manager account, so all the conversion data and audience lists stay with them. The Google Business Profile? Managed under an email the operator never had the password to. The CRM? The agency's seat, so the customer list isn't exportable.

In a week, an operator can lose the website they paid to build, the rankings that took years, the ad data that made their campaigns smart, and the customer list that is the business. Not because they did anything wrong — because nobody told them to ask who owned what on day one.

**Key Takeaway:** The risk isn't bad marketing work. It's discovering, the day you try to leave, that the website, ad accounts, and data you paid for were never in your name.

## What "owning your data" actually means

"You own your data" is a phrase a lot of agencies say and few define. Here's the concrete checklist of what you should own outright, in accounts under your name and control:

- **Your domain name.** Registered to you, in a registrar account you control. This is the deed to your digital storefront.
- **Your website.** The hosting account in your name, plus the actual site files, exportable and portable to any provider.
- **Your Google Business Profile.** Owned by your email, with you as the primary owner — not an agency's account with you as a "manager."
- **Your ad accounts.** Google Ads, Meta, and any others created in your business's name, with the agency added as a manager you can remove at will.
- **Your CRM and customer list.** Your data, exportable any time, in a platform you hold the account for.
- **Your analytics and tracking.** Google Analytics and your tracking pixels under your account, so the historical data stays with you.

The test is simple: for each item, if you parted ways with your agency tomorrow, would it still be yours, with no permission needed from them? If the answer is no for any of these, you don't fully own your marketing — you're renting it. This is the exact principle behind [our Growth System](/growth-system) and the promise on [our pricing page](/pricing): you own the data, you own the assets, we own the results.

**Key Takeaway:** Owning your marketing means the domain, website, GBP, ad accounts, CRM, and analytics all live in accounts under your name — yours to keep the day you leave, no permission required.

## 7 questions to ask before you hire (or ask your current vendor today)

You don't need to be technical to protect yourself. Ask these seven questions and get the answers in writing.

1. **Will the domain be registered in my name, in an account I control?**
2. **Will I own the website hosting and the site files, and can I export them if I leave?**
3. **Will my Google Business Profile be owned by my email, with me as primary owner?**
4. **Will my Google Ads and Meta accounts be created in my business's name, with you added as a manager I can remove?**
5. **Will my CRM and customer list be exportable by me at any time?**
6. **Will Google Analytics and my tracking pixels be under my account?**
7. **When our relationship ends, what exactly do I keep, and is that spelled out in the contract?**

A good partner answers "yes, yes, yes" and points to where it's written down. The right agency wants you to own everything, because their value is the results they produce, not the hostage situation they can create. If a vendor gets cagey on any of these, you've learned something important before you signed, not after.

**Key Takeaway:** Seven plain questions — about your domain, site, GBP, ad accounts, CRM, analytics, and exit terms — reveal whether you'll own your marketing or rent it. Get the answers in writing.

## Red flags in agency contracts

Beyond the questions, watch for these patterns in the agreement itself. Any one is worth pausing over.

- **"Proprietary platform" lock-in.** If your website or CRM only runs on the agency's in-house system, you can't take it anywhere. Your assets are only as portable as the platform they're built on.
- **Vague or missing ownership language.** If the contract doesn't clearly say you own the domain, site, accounts, and data, assume you don't. Silence favors the agency.
- **Offboarding fees.** A charge to "release" your own website, domain, or accounts is a tell. You shouldn't pay a ransom for things that are yours.
- **Agency-owned accounts presented as a convenience.** "We'll just set it all up under our account to make it easy" is how the keys end up in someone else's pocket. Easy on day one, expensive on the day you leave.
- **No clear exit clause.** A contract that describes how you join but not what happens when you leave is missing the part that protects you most.

This is a real enough problem that the [FTC's business guidance on data and privacy](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/privacy-security/data-security) treats clear data practices as a basic obligation, not a nice-to-have. Your marketing data deserves the same scrutiny you'd give any contract that touches the core of your business.

**Key Takeaway:** Proprietary lock-in, vague ownership language, offboarding fees, and "we'll set it up under our account" are the contract patterns that quietly transfer control away from you.

## Common questions

### Do I own my website if my agency built it?

Only if your contract says so and the site lives on accounts in your name. Plenty of agencies build your site on their own hosting, their own template license, or a proprietary platform you can't export. If you leave, the site doesn't come with you. Before you sign, confirm in writing that you own the domain, the hosting account, and the actual site files, and that you can take them to another provider at any time.

### Who owns my Google Ads account?

You should — but many operators don't. If the agency created the Google Ads account under their own manager account rather than setting up an account in your business's name and granting themselves access, they own it, and your entire ad history, conversion data, and audience lists can walk out the door with them. The fix is simple: the ad account should be created in your name, with the agency added as a manager you can remove.

### What happens to my data if I leave my marketing agency?

With a good agency, nothing — you keep your website, domain, Google Business Profile, ad accounts, CRM, analytics, and every asset, because they were always yours. With the wrong one, leaving means losing your site, your leads, your ad history, and your rankings overnight, because everything was built on accounts the agency controls. The difference is entirely in how the relationship was set up on day one, which is why you ask before you sign, not after.
