Roofing lead generation works best when each channel has one job. Google Search Ads and Local Services Ads reach homeowners who need a roofer now. Service pages and storm content catch the searches that follow bad weather. Meta keeps replacement buyers engaged while they take weeks to decide.

This guide covers how to market a roofing company across repair, replacement and storm work. Its roofing marketing ideas are grounded in what BLS, NOAA, the National Roofing Contractors Association and Google have published. It also shows how ASP runs [roofing marketing](/industries/roofing) for contractors who want leads traced to booked jobs wherever their CRM supports it.

## Where Roofing Demand Comes From

The Bureau of Labor Statistics [names](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/construction-and-extraction/roofers.htm) roof replacement, roof repair and new roof installation as what will create demand for roofers. It says some of that demand may come from rooftop solar panel installs. Those are different buyers. A homeowner with a drip in the ceiling wants a fast answer, while one pricing a full replacement compares companies, materials and quotes.

The calendar shapes the work too. BLS notes that roofing work may be limited during the winter months in northern states. It also notes that roofers may work overtime in the busy summer months, and that crews usually do not work in bad weather. Marketing should push hardest when your crews can get on roofs, and the slower months are a good time to line up inspections and planned replacements.

Competition is local. BLS counted 26,626 private residential roofing contractor establishments nationally in 2025. What matters for your plan is how many of them chase the same searches in your zip codes, and how strong their sites already are.

**Key Takeaway:** Treat repair, replacement and new installs as separate buyers, and time your strongest push to the months your crews can work.

## Storms and Hail Season

Storms add sudden spikes on top of the season. NOAA's National Severe Storms Laboratory [says](https://www.nssl.noaa.gov/education/svrwx101/hail/) hail can damage homes, and the area where Nebraska, Colorado and Wyoming meet averages seven to nine hail days a year. In guidance [published in 2013](https://www.professionalroofing.net/Articles/Tech-Today--10-01-2013/2338), the National Roofing Contractors Association recommended inspecting roofs at least twice a year, preferably in the spring and fall. It also suggested extra inspections after severe weather such as a hurricane, tornado or hailstorm.

Evergreen content covers that demand. Pages on hail damage, wind damage and storm inspections, which should be live and indexed well before storm season so they are ready when searches spike. Inspection pages timed to spring and fall also give homeowners a reason to call before a leak forces it.

Missed calls may count against you on Local Services Ads, so call coverage and message handling need to be in place before a storm budget goes up.

**Key Takeaway:** Have storm and inspection pages indexed before the season, and settle call coverage before you raise spend after a storm.

## Google Search Ads and Local Services Ads

Search Ads catch the homeowner who types a problem into Google, like a leak, missing shingles or a replacement quote. Build one ad group per service or service area, use phrase match and grow the negative keyword list every week, so you can see whether repairs or replacements are paying. Roofing accounts need their own negatives too, such as searches from people looking for roofing jobs, pricing shingles to buy or planning a do-it-yourself repair. Weight the budget toward the services you most want to sell.

Local Services Ads add a pay-per-lead option. Google [lists](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/6224841?hl=en) roofers as a Local Services Ads category in the US, and leads come in as phone calls, messages or bookings made through the ad. Before any ads go live, Google screens the business.

Rank on these ads is not just a bidding contest. Google's [ad rankings page](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/7527305?hl=en) weighs your bid, how likely your ad is to produce a lead, the context of the search and your profile quality. Profile quality includes your rating, review count, response time, photos and completed verification checks. Every review should come from a real customer and fall within Google's review policies and the FTC's rule on fake reviews. Keep incentives, quotas and scripts out of it. Google now calls its badge Google Verified. Google says it will discontinue the money-back guarantee tied to the older badge, so ad copy shouldn't be built around it. Our [roofing advertising page](/ppc/roofing) covers account setup.

**Key Takeaway:** Split Search Ads by service with roofing-specific negatives, and treat reviews and response time as ranking factors alongside your Local Services Ads bid.

## Service Pages and the Map Pack

Map-pack placement leans on your Business Profile, review volume and recency, and consistent citations. Google's [Business Profile guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177?hl=en) say a business serving customers at their location should have one profile for its central office with a designated service area. A virtual office can't be listed unless it is staffed during business hours. An unstaffed address in each nearby town breaks those rules, so service-area pages on your website cover those towns instead. [Local SEO Pro](/local-seo-pro) handles the profile, citation audits and a monthly ranking heatmap, and our [local SEO guide](/blog/local-seo-for-home-service-businesses) walks through the profile basics.

On the website, build content clusters in order of what a booked job is worth to you. A replacement page, for example, can be surrounded by a material comparison, a cost breakdown and the signs a roof needs replacing, all linked together. A service with real search demand gets its own page, and one nobody searches for by name gets folded into a broader page.

The detail that ranks comes from your crew. Record interviews with you or a senior crew member about things only a roofer knows. Good topics include what a tear-off cost last month and why a patch fails on an aging roof. Put named authors and their real credentials on the page. If your site carries large project galleries or before-and-after photos, keep them, and size them so pages still load fast on a phone. Our [roofing SEO services](/seo/roofing) page covers the full organic plan.

**Key Takeaway:** Keep one Business Profile inside Google's rules, and build replacement and repair pages from details only your crew can give.

## Meta Ads for Replacement Buyers and Crew Hiring

Facebook and Instagram fit roof replacement because the decision takes weeks, which gives finished-roof photos and video time to work. Cold ads asking a stranger to book a leak repair rarely pay off. Start by retargeting homeowners who read your replacement page and left without calling. Use creative from your own jobs, like before-and-after shots or a short answer to a question you hear every week.

Meta also helps with hiring. BLS counted about 166,900 roofer jobs in 2025 and projects about 12,000 openings a year, on average, through 2035. Recruiting campaigns should run on their own budget so hiring spend is never read as lead performance.

**Key Takeaway:** Point Meta at replacement buyers and crew hiring, and leave emergency leak calls to search.

## Tracking Roofing Leads to Booked Jobs

A repair lead and a replacement lead can be worth very different amounts. A report that only counts leads hides which channel is paying. Set the conversion action to the lead you sell and keep it there, even if the account learns more slowly. Clean up duplicate conversions and short calls counted as jobs, so a bad month doesn't look like a good one. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is roofing lead generation?

Roofing lead generation is the work of bringing in calls and form fills from homeowners who need roof repair, replacement or a storm inspection. It usually combines Google Search Ads, Local Services Ads, local SEO, storm content and Meta. The goal is booked jobs, so each channel should be judged on the revenue it produces, not the lead count.

### How do roofers get leads after a hailstorm?

The best position is having pages and ads ready before the storm. Hail and storm-damage pages that are indexed before the season can show up when searches spike, and weather-triggered posts can add event content for your service area. In 2013 guidance, the National Roofing Contractors Association suggested extra inspections after severe weather, which gives you an honest offer to lead with.

### Are Local Services Ads worth it for roofing companies?

For roofers who qualify, they are worth testing because you pay per lead instead of per click. Google lists roofers as a US category. It ranks ads on your bid, how likely the ad is to bring a lead and your profile quality, including rating, review count and response time. Run them alongside Search Ads rather than instead of them.

### Do Facebook ads work for roofing companies?

For replacements, often yes, because homeowners take weeks to decide and photos of finished roofs help. For emergency leaks, rarely, since those homeowners search Google the moment water comes through the ceiling. Start with retargeting people who already visited your replacement page, and run any crew recruiting on a separate budget.

### Should roofing ads run the same budget all year?

Usually not. BLS notes that roofing work may be limited in winter in northern states. It also notes that roofers may work overtime in the busy summer months and that crews usually do not work in bad weather. Budgets should follow the months your crews can take the work and ease off when the schedule is already full.

### How much should a roofer spend on Google Ads?

Work back from what a booked job is worth to you, not from a flat monthly figure. A replacement can still pay even with a much higher cost per lead than a small repair. Ad spend goes directly to Google from your own account, separate from management fees, and the budget should lean toward the services you most want to sell.
