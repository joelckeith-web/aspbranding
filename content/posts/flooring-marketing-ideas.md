The flooring marketing ideas to start with are service pages built for search and Local Services Ads where Google offers them. They also include Facebook and Instagram ads that show finished rooms, and tracking that follows each lead to an installed job. Search comes first because the homeowner typing a flooring search already wants the work done. Flooring is also decided over weeks, not minutes, which gives photos of finished rooms time to work.

This guide is for flooring installers and showrooms deciding where to put their marketing time and budget. It draws on figures from the Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS), the Census Bureau and Harvard's Joint Center for Housing Studies (JCHS). It also draws on how we run [flooring company marketing](/industries/flooring) at ASP.

## Where Flooring Demand Comes From

BLS expects new-home construction and the renovation of existing homes to be the primary source of flooring and tile installation work through 2035. Its [Occupational Outlook Handbook](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/construction-and-extraction/tile-and-marble-setters.htm) projects employment of flooring installers and tile and stone setters to grow 4 percent from 2025 to 2035. That is a projection of employment in the trade, not a count of flooring projects. We have not found a primary source that tracks flooring demand by season, so your own lead and job history is the best guide to timing.

Homeowners are also hiring out more of their projects. JCHS covers this in [Improving America's Housing 2025](https://www.jchs.harvard.edu/sites/default/files/reports/files/Harvard_JCHS_Improving_Americas_Housing_2025.pdf). It reports that from 2021 to 2023, owner spending on projects that often need skilled labor grew faster than the improvement market overall. Those projects include interior replacements such as flooring. Over the same years, spending on professionally installed projects rose 14 percent in real terms, while do-it-yourself spending fell 13 percent.

Older owners lean toward replacement work. In 2023, owners age 65 and over spent an average of 54 percent of their improvement dollars on replacement projects such as flooring. For owners under 65, the figure was 47 percent. Write for that buyer as well as younger ones, with clear answers on cost, durability and what install day looks like.

Most flooring contractor businesses with employees are small. Census counted 17,989 flooring contractor establishments with paid employees in 2023, and 13,809 of them had fewer than five employees. Those counts leave out owner-only businesses with no payroll. If your company runs real crews, show it with photos of your team, finished jobs and a clear explanation of how a project runs from measure to install.

**Key Takeaway:** From 2021 to 2023, spending on professionally installed projects grew while DIY spending fell. In 2023, older owners put more of their budgets into replacements such as flooring. Your marketing should answer cost, durability and install-day questions plainly.

## Search Pages for Each Flooring Service

Search comes first, because the homeowner typing "hardwood floor refinishing near me" already wants the work done. Google's own description of a flooring professional covers installation, repair, sanding and refinishing, and those make good starting points for your service pages. Build a page for each service that has real demand, in order of what a booked job in that service is worth to you.

One page per service is only the start. A flooring service page gets stronger when it links to a cost breakdown, a comparison of materials or methods, and answers to the questions buyers ask before they book. The details should come from your installers. They include what drives the price in your market, which subfloor problems change the job, and how long a room is out of use. Our [home service SEO](/seo) work builds these clusters in order of what a booked job is worth, not in order of search volume.

BLS says certification is not required for flooring installers, so a voluntary credential is worth showing when your team has one. The National Wood Flooring Association runs a [Certified Professional program](https://nwfa.org/cp-about/) and says many of its member manufacturers recommend hiring NWFA Certified Professionals.

**Key Takeaway:** Build pages for the flooring services people search for, in order of what each job is worth, and fill them with details only your installers know.

## Local Services Ads and Your Business Profile

Flooring services is an eligible category for [Local Services Ads](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/6224841?hl=en) in the US, where Google offers them in your area. These ads charge by the lead rather than the click, and Google screens every business before its ads run.

Google ranks these ads in an auction. According to its [page on ad rankings](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/7527305?hl=en), Google weighs your bid, how likely your ad is to produce a lead, and profile quality. Profile quality covers your rating, review count, response time, photos and the verification checks you have completed. If you run more than one showroom, keep in mind that when several of your locations serve the same area, Google shows only your highest-ranking ad.

Your Google Business Profile is what shows in the map results. Keep services, service areas and hours current, and add photos of finished floors from your own jobs. Our [local SEO guide for home service businesses](/blog/local-seo-for-home-service-businesses) goes deeper on the profile.

Reviews show on your profile and count toward Local Services Ads profile quality. Ask every customer the same way after the install, with a review link in your follow-up text or email, and reply to what comes in. Google's review policies prohibit offering free or discounted goods or services in exchange for reviews. The FTC's rule on fake reviews and testimonials bans fake reviews. It also bans incentives tied to a positive or negative review. Don't set quotas or script what customers say, either.

**Key Takeaway:** Local Services Ads rank on profile quality as well as bid, so reviews, photos and fast responses affect where your ads show.

## Facebook and Instagram Ads for Flooring

Flooring is decided over weeks, not minutes, and that is where Facebook and Instagram ads earn a place. A homeowner pricing new floors has time to look at finished rooms, before-and-after photos and short videos of your crew before filling out a form. Meta, the company behind both apps, sells them through one ad account.

The order of the work matters. Search should already be working. If people in your area search for flooring and find someone else, fix that before Facebook gets more budget. Retargeting people who visited your flooring pages without calling goes live before money goes to cold audiences. The offer should fit a long decision, such as an in-home measure or a showroom visit.

Creative should come from your own jobs. These are the pieces we plan for flooring accounts:

- Before-and-after photos of finished rooms
- A short walk-through of a completed install
- An installer answering one question customers ask every week
- Vertical video shot for feeds and Reels, not cropped from landscape footage

Once lead and customer data build up, lookalike audiences can be built from real buyers rather than all site traffic. That is what makes cold audiences worth testing later. Our [Meta ads management](/meta-ads) page covers how we run these accounts.

**Key Takeaway:** Use Facebook and Instagram to keep finished work in front of buyers who are still deciding, after search is in place and retargeting is running.

## Tracking Installed Jobs

A flooring lead is not a sale. The path runs from a call or form, to a measure or estimate, to a signed job and then the install. Your reporting has to follow it the whole way. Call tracking numbers and form fields record where each lead came from, and that source is carried into your CRM so you can see installed jobs and revenue by channel. Our [guide to marketing attribution](/blog/marketing-attribution-home-service-businesses) explains how those pieces connect.

Where your CRM supports it, booked revenue is sent back to Google Ads as an offline conversion. Bidding then follows the leads that turn into jobs instead of every lead that calls. A slow reply to an estimate request can lose the job along the way, which our [speed to lead guide](/blog/speed-to-lead-home-service-businesses) covers.

Crew capacity matters as much as lead volume. JCHS reports that projected job gains will not close the skilled-trade deficit for flooring installers. For that reason, we weight the budget toward the services and areas your crews can take on. Leads your crews can't install still cost money.
