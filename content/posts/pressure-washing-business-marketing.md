Pressure washing business marketing comes down to two jobs. You need to get found on Google when a homeowner searches. Then you need to show enough of your work that they book you. That means a complete Google Business Profile, a page for each surface you clean and Google Search Ads. It also means before-and-after video on Facebook and Instagram, and tracking that ties each lead to a booked job.

Federal data doesn't track pressure washing on its own. So this guide uses the closest federal category, with its limits, plus what Google and the trade's own associations have published. It also covers how ASP runs [pressure washing marketing](/industries/pressure-washing) for house wash, roof cleaning and flatwork companies, and where each channel fits.

## Demand and Seasonality

The trade has no NAICS code of its own. The closest federal category is [Other Services to Buildings and Dwellings](https://www.census.gov/naics/resources/model/dataHandler.php?input=561790&chart=2022&search=Y), which lists building exterior cleaning and drain or gutter cleaning as examples. The same category also includes pool, chimney and duct cleaning, so any figure for it describes more than pressure washing.

Employment in that broader category moves with the season. US private employment was 103,283 in January 2024 and 131,517 in July 2024. Those are worker counts, not a measure of customer demand. And because pool cleaning sits in the same group, the swing can't be pinned on pressure washing alone.

The practical answer is to plan from your own history. Past invoices, call logs and booked jobs show which months fill up in your market. Spend should lead into your busy months and pull back when crews are booked. Once tracking has run for a season, the ad schedule can come from tracked jobs by month.

**Key Takeaway:** National data can't tell you when your phone rings, so build the marketing calendar from your own invoices and booked jobs.

## Google Business Profile and the Map Pack

Google's [help page on local ranking](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091?hl=en) names relevance, distance and prominence as the main inputs for local results. It also states that a better local ranking can't be requested or paid for. Prominence depends partly on how many websites link to a business and how many reviews it has.

The profile rules matter for a business that travels to customers. Google's [Business Profile guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177?hl=en) allow a service-area business with no storefront one profile. They also say the service area shouldn't extend farther than about 2 hours of driving from where the business is based. Google adds that a larger area may suit some businesses, and separate locations with their own staff and service areas can each have a profile.

A strong profile for this trade lists each service accurately, carries before-and-after photos of your own jobs and gets regular posts. ASP's [Local SEO Pro](/local-seo-pro) includes weekly Business Profile posts, citation cleanup and a monthly ranking heatmap. Our [local SEO playbook](/blog/local-seo-for-home-service-businesses) covers the profile in more depth.

Reviews feed prominence and give a homeowner comparing quotes a reason to trust you. Send the request in your follow-up after each job with a direct Google review link. Stay inside Google's review guidelines and the FTC's rule on fake reviews. Keep the request simple, with no discount or freebie in exchange, no quota for your crews and no suggested wording.

**Key Takeaway:** An accurate service-area profile, your own before-and-after photos and steady honest reviews are the base every other channel builds on.

## Service Pages for Each Surface

A homeowner looking for roof cleaning and one trying to clean a stained driveway want different answers. Give house washing, roof cleaning, concrete and flatwork, and decks and fences their own pages. Each page should answer what a buyer wants to know before calling: the method, the surfaces it suits, what drives the price and what the result looks like.

Build pages in order of what a booked job is worth to you rather than search volume alone. A service with steady demand and good margin earns a full page, while one nobody searches for by name can sit inside a broader page. The best details come from you or a senior tech, such as how you protect plants and painted trim, or when you choose low pressure over high.

Fix technical problems before adding content. Pages Google can't index, redirect chains and slow mobile pages come first. Our [home service SEO](/seo) page explains how that work gets sequenced and reported against booked jobs.

**Key Takeaway:** Give each surface you clean its own page, written with the first-hand detail a generic competitor page can't copy.

## Google Search Ads and Local Services Ads

Google's Local Services Ads only run in certain categories. When the full [US category list](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/6224841?hl=en) was checked on October 15, 2026, it had no pressure washing or power washing category. Nearby categories include Window cleaning services and House cleaning services.

In Google's [description of window cleaning](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/12174778?hl=en), the category covers cleaning windows, mirrors, skylights and gutters. Whether a pressure washing company qualifies there is Google's call, so it isn't something to assume. Google presents the list as what is available "at this time," so it is worth rechecking. Until it changes, Google Search Ads do the paid work of catching people who are already looking.

Give each service its own ad group: house washing, roof cleaning, driveways and concrete, decks and fences. That separation shows which service is booking jobs, so budget can move toward it. Phrase match and a negative keyword list that grows every week keep spend away from searches you don't want, such as equipment rentals and how-to questions.

Read the search terms report on a schedule. It shows what people typed to trigger your ads, and it's the cheapest win in most accounts and the one most often skipped. Set ad geography to the area your crews will drive to, and answer calls fast, which our piece on [speed to lead](/blog/speed-to-lead-home-service-businesses) explains. Our [pressure washing advertising](/ppc/pressure-washing) page covers the full account setup.

**Key Takeaway:** With no Local Services Ads category as of October 2026, a clean Search Ads account built by service does the paid work.

## Facebook and Instagram Ads

Pressure washing is a visual job, and your own work supplies the best creative. Before-and-after sequences are one of the formats we lean on for the trades. Vertical video built as vertical video outperforms cropped landscape footage in feeds and Reels.

Recurring work, like a yearly house wash, can carry a seasonal offer directly. Start with retargeting people who visited your site before spending on cold audiences. Keep the ad radius inside your service area so spend doesn't drift across a metro.

Cold ads asking a stranger to book today rarely work for the trades. Facebook works best once search is already catching the demand that exists. Our [Meta ads](/meta-ads) page covers creative and audiences in more detail.

**Key Takeaway:** Use your own before-and-after video, start with retargeting and keep Facebook in support of search.

## Certifications Buyers Can See

Voluntary certifications give a homeowner comparing quotes a specific reason to trust you. The [Power Washers of North America](https://www.pwna.org/about-us), a non-profit trade association founded in 1992, reports issuing more than 7,000 certifications. Its [House Wash Certification](https://www.pwna.org/certificate-courses/house-wash-certification) teaches contractors to clean homes with low-pressure methods, covering chemical handling and surface protection.

The [United Association of Mobile Contract Cleaners](https://www.uamcc.org/education/certification/) offers certifications including Roof Cleaning and Wood Restoration. These are association certifications, not licenses, so their value comes from showing them clearly. Put the ones your techs hold on your service pages, your Business Profile and your ad copy.

**Key Takeaway:** If your crew holds a certification, show it where homeowners compare quotes.

## Tracking Booked Jobs and Repeat Customers

Report calls, forms, booked jobs and revenue by channel, not clicks alone. Tracking numbers tag each call by source. Forms pass their source into your CRM so it stays attached to the job where your CRM supports it. Keep the ad account's conversion action set to the lead you sell, and don't swap it for a softer event to get through the learning phase faster.

Repeat customers need their own rule. A homeowner who books a house wash every year was won once. Later visits count as retention instead of new acquisition, which keeps each channel's numbers honest.
