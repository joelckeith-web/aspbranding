Water damage restoration marketing has to reach a property owner while the damage is fresh and they are deciding who to call. That means being easy to find on Google and showing the standards and certifications you work to. It also means answering the phone fast and tracking each lead to a booked job.

In this trade, timing matters. EPA advises [drying water-damaged areas and items within 24 to 48 hours](https://www.epa.gov/mold/brief-guide-mold-moisture-and-your-home) to prevent mold growth, which leaves a homeowner little time to shop around. This guide covers restoration lead generation for water, fire and mold work, and how ASP runs [restoration marketing](/industries/restoration) against booked jobs.

## When Homeowners Call a Professional

EPA guidance sends bigger mold and contaminated-water jobs to professionals. Its [mold cleanup guide](https://www.epa.gov/mold/mold-cleanup-your-home) says that in most cases a homeowner can handle a moldy patch under about 10 square feet. For larger water damage or mold beyond that, it points to its professional remediation guide. It also advises calling a professional when the damage came from sewage or other contaminated water.

Those are the jobs your marketing should be built for. With so little time to dry things out, being easy to find and quick to answer carries a lot of weight.

That makes response part of the marketing. A missed call can send the homeowner to the next company on the list, and our piece on [speed to lead](/blog/speed-to-lead-home-service-businesses) looks at what that costs. Make the phone number easy to tap on every page, and avoid promising a response time you cannot meet every time.

**Key Takeaway:** Build your marketing around the large and contaminated jobs EPA sends to professionals, and treat answering the phone as part of the plan.

## Storm and Seasonal Demand

Some restoration demand follows the calendar. In markets touched by the Atlantic hurricane season, the [official season](https://www.nhc.noaa.gov/climo/) runs June 1 to November 30, with most activity between mid-August and mid-October. On the fire side, the US Fire Administration [estimates 343,000 residential building fires in 2024](https://www.usfa.fema.gov/statistics/), though not every fire leads to restoration work.

Weather-driven demand rewards content that is ready before the event. Your core service pages should already be live and indexed before storm season, so they are ready when searches spike.

Use your own job history to see how much of your work follows storms and how much comes from other causes. Then plan spend around that mix.

**Key Takeaway:** Have your service pages live before storm season, and let storm-timed content add to them when weather hits your area.

## Google Business Profile and Local Search

Your Google Business Profile has to match how you operate. Google's [Business Profile guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177?hl=en) allow a service-area business one profile, or one per location when each location has its own service area and staff. The service area should not extend farther than about two hours of driving from where you are based. Google does say larger areas can suit some businesses.

Fill the profile out completely: the right primary category, each service you want calls for, accurate hours and photos of your own crews and equipment. List after-hours service only if a person picks up the phone after hours.

Review volume and recency carry real weight in the map results, and reviews also feed Local Services Ads ranking, covered below. Ask every customer through your follow-up with a direct Google review link, and stay inside Google's review guidelines and the FTC's rule on fake reviews. Never offer a discount for a review, never set crew quotas and never script what customers say.

ASP's [Local SEO Pro](/local-seo-pro) handles profile management, citation cleanup and a monthly ranking heatmap across your service area. Our [local SEO playbook for home service businesses](/blog/local-seo-for-home-service-businesses) goes deeper on the profile.

**Key Takeaway:** Keep the profile accurate, including hours, and build a steady flow of honest reviews, since they count in both the map and Local Services Ads.

## Service Pages for Water, Fire and Mold

Water damage, mold remediation, and fire and smoke damage are different jobs, and each needs its own page. IICRC publishes a [separate ANSI standard for each one](https://iicrc.org/iicrcstandards/): S500 for water damage, S520 for mold and S700 for fire and smoke. EPA tells homeowners hiring a mold contractor to check experience and references. It also tells them to ask that the work follow EPA, ACGIH, IICRC or other professional or government guidelines. A page that names the standards you follow answers that question before the call.

Some content earns trust by being straight with people. FEMA's flood insurance program [states that most homeowners insurance does not cover flood damage](https://www.floodsmart.gov/). A page that explains this plainly, without promising what any policy will pay, helps a homeowner understand where they stand.

Fill each page with what only your crews know. That includes what you check first, how you decide what can be dried and what has to come out, and what drives the cost. Build pages in order of what a booked job is worth to you. Fix technical problems, like pages Google can't index or slow mobile load times, before adding more.

**Key Takeaway:** Give water, fire and mold their own pages, name the standards you work to, and explain insurance basics without making promises.

## Local Services Ads

Google's US Local Services Ads list includes a [water damage services category](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/12174778?hl=en). Google describes it as cleaning and inspecting water damage issues such as mold, sewage and paint damage. Google screens every business before ads run, and for this category the screening includes IICRC certification. That makes the certification worth showing on your site as well as a requirement for this ad placement.

Placement comes from an auction. Google's [help page on ad rankings](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/7527305?hl=en) says it weighs your bid and your profile quality. Profile quality includes your rating, number of reviews, average response time, photos and completed verification checks. The same page says missed calls may hurt your responsiveness, so missed calls can cost you position.

As of October 15, 2026, the US list had no separate category for fire and smoke damage or for mold-only work. Even so, Google's description of the water damage category does mention mold. For fire and mold work beyond that, Google Search Ads cover the ground.

**Key Takeaway:** If you hold IICRC certification and pass Google's other screening checks, you can run Local Services Ads for water damage. Answered calls and fresh reviews help your position.

## Google Search Ads and Meta

Search Ads catch the property owner typing a specific problem into Google. Give each service its own ad group so you can see which line is paying, use phrase match, and add to the negative keyword list every week. Keep the conversion action set to a real lead, so reports count real leads and not page views. Our [PPC management](/ppc) page lays out the account structure.

Meta plays a different role. Emergency work gets searched for at the moment it happens. Facebook and Instagram are better used for two other jobs. One is making your name familiar in the service area beforehand, and the other is bringing back people who visited your site. Our [Meta ads](/meta-ads) page explains how that retargeting works.

**Key Takeaway:** Search Ads catch the emergency, and Meta builds familiarity before it happens.
