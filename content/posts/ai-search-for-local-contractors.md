On October 8, 2026, we ran a set of local contractor searches in Google AI Mode, Google AI Overviews and Perplexity. We wanted to see what AI search for local contractors shows a customer who asks for a recommendation or a price. For "best" searches, AI Mode named highly rated local companies and showed their star ratings. For one Seattle HVAC search, Perplexity cited only lists and directories. For one Austin cost search, an AI Overview tied its price ranges to local contractors' own pages, most of them cost pages. Each result is one query on one day, so read it as a snapshot and not as a rule.

ASP works with a home inspection company in the Seattle area and an epoxy flooring company in New Orleans, and both showed up in the AI Mode answers we checked. If you want the strategy behind answer engine optimization (AEO), our [full guide to AEO for home service businesses](/blog/ai-optimization-aeo-home-service-businesses-2025) covers it. This post sticks to what appeared on the screen and how it fits into a [complete growth system for a local business](/growth-system).

## How we ran the AI search checks on October 8, 2026

All of the searches in this post ran on October 8, 2026, in the Chrome browser. Google was signed in to an ASP account, and Perplexity was used while logged out. For each answer, we recorded the businesses it named, the sources it showed, or both. Some source panels had more entries behind a "Show all" button, and we note below where we did not record them.

We did not capture ChatGPT or Gemini. AI chat accounts carry memory and personalization, so answers from our own account could reflect our past use instead of what a typical customer would see. Signed-in Google can personalize too. Google's [help page for AI Mode in Google Search](https://support.google.com/websearch/answer/16011537) addresses this. It says that "Personal Intelligence in AI Mode references previous searches and activity saved in your Search Services History to provide suggestions tailored to your tastes and preferences." We did not record whether that setting was on, so the Google results here may include some personalization.

Here is what we looked at:

## What Google AI Overviews and AI Mode are

An AI Overview is the AI-written summary that can appear in regular Google results. Google's [help page for AI Overviews](https://support.google.com/websearch/answer/14901683) describes it as "an AI-generated snapshot with key information and links to dig deeper." It does not appear for every search. Google's [guide to AI features for site owners](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features) says AI Overviews show only when its systems decide they add something to classic Search, "and as such, often don't trigger."

AI Mode is a separate, conversational search experience inside Google. Google calls it "Google's most powerful AI search experience" and says it gives "an AI-powered response, with the ability to go deeper through follow-up questions and helpful links to the web." Google [introduced AI Mode as an early experiment in its Labs program on March 5, 2025](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/search/ai-mode-search/).

Both features can show links to the pages behind an answer. In this post, the "sources panel" means the list of linked pages shown with an AI answer. Google says both features may use a "query fan-out" technique, "issuing multiple related searches across subtopics and data sources," to build a response. Google also says AI Mode and AI Overviews "may use different models and techniques, so the set of responses and links they show will vary." That point comes up again in the furnace example below.

Perplexity is a separate AI search tool. Its [help center article on how Perplexity works](https://www.perplexity.ai/help-center/en/articles/10352895-how-does-perplexity-work) says it searches "the internet in real-time" and that "each answer includes numbered citations linking to the original sources."

## What Perplexity cited for the same Seattle HVAC search

Perplexity, used while logged out, named two HVAC companies for "best HVAC company in Seattle." One of them also appeared in the AI Mode answer. The two tools showed different review counts for that company. AI Mode showed 4.9K reviews, while Perplexity cited a 2026 ranking that listed 4.9 stars and 4,306 reviews. We report both numbers as they appeared.

Perplexity listed 10 sources, and all 10 were lists or directories. One was a top-10 list on a website's blog, and we have not established who owns that site. The visible AI Mode sources for the same question were a contractor website and two business profiles. On this one day, the two tools answered the same question from very different kinds of pages.

We would not read this as a reason to buy placements on ranking sites or to chase directory listings. One query cannot tell you how Perplexity chooses its sources, and it does not show that Perplexity cites only directories.

**Key Takeaway:** In one logged-out Perplexity search, all 10 cited sources were lists or directories, and only one of the two HVAC companies Perplexity named also appeared in Google AI Mode.

## Where the AI Overview for Austin outdoor kitchen costs got its prices

This cost question brought up a different kind of source. For "how much does an outdoor kitchen cost in Austin," the AI Overview said a custom outdoor kitchen in Austin typically ranges from $20,000 to $60,000. It then split the price into tiers, from a basic grill island up to a complete outdoor living suite. Those are the AI Overview's figures, not ours, and they are not a quote for any project.

<!-- Screenshot: AI Overview for "how much does an outdoor kitchen cost in Austin", Oct 8, 2026 -->

We recorded the first seven entries in the sources panel. Five were pages from Texas contractors, and four of those five were cost pages. The other two entries were videos, and more sources may have followed the first seven. The answer tied its price ranges to the contractor pages through citation chips, the small source links that sit beside a line of the answer.

So in this search, the price ranges the AI Overview showed were tied to local contractors' own pages, four of which were cost pages. Google's guide says, "There are no additional requirements to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode, nor other special optimizations necessary." A clear cost page on your own site is regular SEO work, and in this one search it was the kind of page that got cited.

**Key Takeaway:** In one Austin cost search, the AI Overview tied its price ranges to Texas contractors' own pages, and five of the first seven sources were contractor pages.

## Furnace service answers from Google AI Overviews and AI Mode

We asked both Google features "how often should a furnace be serviced." This query did not include a city. The AI Overview said, "You should have your furnace professionally serviced at least once a year," and pointed to early fall or late summer for timing. AI Mode said, "A furnace should be serviced once every year, ideally in the fall before the cold weather sets in."

The two answers agreed, but their sources did not. The AI Overview showed six sources: an online forum, an HVAC manufacturer, one HVAC contractor's website and three other websites we did not classify. AI Mode showed no cited sources for this answer, and a sponsored HVAC ad appeared below it. The two features shared no sources.

<!-- Screenshot: AI Overview and AI Mode answers for "how often should a furnace be serviced", Oct 8, 2026 -->

This does not mean AI Mode leaves out sources as a rule. Google's guide says AI Mode gives a response "with links to supporting websites." Google's help page adds a related point. It says that "in some cases, AI Mode will provide a set of web links if there's not high enough confidence in the quality or helpfulness of an AI response." The result is consistent with Google's note that the two features "may use different models and techniques, so the set of responses and links they show will vary."

**Key Takeaway:** For the same furnace question on the same day, the AI Overview cited six sources and AI Mode cited none, so on this question the two Google AI features did not share a single source.

## What AI search for local contractors showed us

These checks don't prove how any AI tool ranks businesses. Google's own guidance for its AI features points back to standard SEO. Its guide says "the best practices for SEO remain relevant for AI features in Google Search." It also says that to appear as a supporting link, "a page must be indexed and eligible to be shown in Google Search with a snippet."

Google also says site owners don't need special files or markup: "You don't need to create new machine readable files, AI text files, or markup to appear in these features. There's also no special schema.org structured data that you need to add." If someone offers to set up an AI file or special markup to get your business into AI answers, read that line first.

Based on what we saw and what Google states, these are reasonable places to focus:

- **Keep your Google Business Profile accurate.** Google lists up-to-date Business Profile information as an SEO fundamental, and profiles showed up as AI Mode sources in our Seattle HVAC check.
- **Answer the questions customers ask, including cost.** In the Austin check, the AI Overview tied its price ranges to local contractors' own pages, and four of those pages were cost pages.
- **Make sure Google can find and index your pages.** Google lists "making your content easily findable through internal links on your website" as a fundamental.
- **Check AI answers for your own services and city from time to time.** Treat each answer as one snapshot, and remember Google's advice to "always check important info in more than one place."

None of this guarantees a spot. Google says, "Just because a page meets all requirements, best practices, and complies with the policies, doesn't mean that Google will crawl, index, or serve its content." On the measurement side, Google says sites appearing in AI Overviews and AI Mode "are included in the overall search traffic in Search Console."

**Key Takeaway:** The steps that fit both our checks and Google's guidance are ordinary ones: an accurate Business Profile, pages that answer real customer questions, and a site Google can index, with no promise of placement.
