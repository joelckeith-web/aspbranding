The remodeling marketing ideas to start with cover pages, ads and tracking. Build a page for each project type you want more of. Run Local Services Ads where your category is offered, along with Google Search Ads. Add Facebook and Instagram ads for longer decisions, and track each signed project to its source. Together they carry a homeowner from a first search to a signed contract. That path often takes several visits across more than one channel.

This guide is for kitchen, bath and exterior remodeling firms, including larger replacement companies that run several product lines. It draws on research from Harvard's Joint Center for Housing Studies (JCHS) and Google's own documentation, plus how we approach [remodeling marketing](/industries/remodeling) at ASP.

## Where Remodeling Demand Comes From

Home improvement is a large market. In its [March 2025 press release](https://www.jchs.harvard.edu/sites/default/files/interactive-item/files/Harvard_JCHS_Improving_Americas_Housing_2025_Press_Release.pdf), JCHS reported that US home improvement and repair spending rose from $404 billion in 2019 to $611 billion in 2022. It said that spending was expected to stay above $600 billion through 2025. Homeowners spent an average of almost $4,700 on improvements in 2023.

Replacement work carries much of that spending. Projects such as roofing, windows and HVAC made up 49 percent of homeowner improvement spending in 2023. According to the full [Improving America's Housing 2025 report](https://www.jchs.harvard.edu/sites/default/files/reports/files/Harvard_JCHS_Improving_Americas_Housing_2025.pdf), owners spent $46 billion replacing roofs that same year. They also spent $22 billion on windows and doors and $9 billion replacing siding. JCHS groups all of these as energy-related projects.

How a job gets paid for shapes the sale. Homeowners paid for 10 percent of their 2023 exterior replacement spending with home equity and 18 percent with insurance settlements. If you offer financing or handle insurance work, your pages should explain how it works in plain terms.

We have not found a primary source that tracks remodeling demand by season. Your own lead and project history, broken out by product line, is the better guide to when to push each one.

**Key Takeaway:** Replacement projects such as roofing, windows and siding carry a large share of homeowner spending. Part of exterior replacement spending is paid with home equity or insurance, so your pages should explain both.

## Project Pages That Do the Selling

Remodeling buyers research before they call, so your website carries much of the sale. Build a page for each project type you want more of that buyers search for, such as kitchens, baths, siding or windows. Decide the order by demand first, then by what a signed project is worth, then by how strong the pages already ranking are.

A project page should answer the questions a homeowner has while deciding. Add cost breakdowns, side-by-side comparisons of materials or approaches, and galleries of finished projects from your own jobs. The details that make these pages useful come from you and your project leads. Examples are what drives the price in your market and what can change a job once work begins.

Our [home service SEO](/seo) work builds these pages in clusters, in order of what a signed job is worth rather than search volume. Fast replies matter as well, because a homeowner who asks for an estimate and hears nothing may move on to the next firm. Our [speed to lead guide](/blog/speed-to-lead-home-service-businesses) explains why.

**Key Takeaway:** Give each project type you want more of its own page, built with real costs, comparisons and photos from your own work.

## Local Services Ads and Google Search Ads

Google's [Local Services Ads categories](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/6224841?hl=en) in the US include bathroom remodeling, kitchen remodeling, general contractor, roofers, siding services and window repair services. But Google doesn't offer them in every area. Google screens every business before its ads run, and its [screening and verification page](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/12174778?hl=en) has an entry for each category. Check whether your categories are offered in your area before you plan around them.

Google Search Ads reach the homeowner searching for a specific project right now, such as a bathroom remodel or replacement windows. Group campaigns by project type so each ad leads to the matching page, and put more budget behind the projects with the best value per signed job. Our [PPC management](/ppc) page covers how we run search campaigns against booked jobs.

Keep your Google Business Profile current with the project types you do and photos of finished work. Our [local SEO guide for home service businesses](/blog/local-seo-for-home-service-businesses) has the details. Ask every customer for a review the same way when the project wraps up, with a link in your follow-up text or email. Google's review policies prohibit offering free or discounted goods or services in exchange for reviews. The FTC's rule on fake reviews and testimonials bans fake reviews and incentives tied to a positive or negative review. Don't set quotas or script what customers say, either.

**Key Takeaway:** Use Local Services Ads where your category is offered and Search Ads split by project type. Keep a profile and reviews that hold up when buyers check you.

## Facebook and Instagram Ads for Remodeling

Facebook and Instagram ads fit remodeling because the decision takes weeks. That gives photos and video of finished projects time to work. The offer should match a long decision, such as a design consultation or a real estimate.

The order of the work matters. Search should already be catching the demand that exists before Meta gets more budget. Retargeting homeowners who visited your project pages without calling goes live before any spend on cold audiences. Once you have lead and customer data, lookalike audiences built from real buyers make cold audiences worth testing.

Creative should come from your own projects. Examples include before-and-after sequences, a walk-through of a finished kitchen or bath, and a project lead answering a question homeowners ask every week. Shoot vertical video for feeds and Reels rather than cropping landscape footage. Our [Meta ads management](/meta-ads) page covers how we build these accounts.

**Key Takeaway:** Run Facebook and Instagram after search is working, start with retargeting, and offer the consultation or estimate that a long decision calls for.

## Marketing a Larger Remodeling Firm

Remodeling is still a fragmented business. In the 2017 Economic Census, the 50 largest firms held under 8 percent of general remodeler receipts. By 2023, the number of general remodelers with payrolls passed 125,000. Even so, general remodelers with revenues of at least $2.5 million grew their share of industry receipts from 45 percent in 2007 to 57 percent in 2017.

JCHS says specialty replacement firms are driving that concentration. Companies focused on roofing, siding, windows and quick-turn bath remodels made up 57 percent of the top 500 remodelers in 2023, up from 43 percent in 2013. For a firm like that, marketing runs as a volume system by product line, with each line tracked to signed jobs.

Crews set the ceiling. In 2023, 64 percent of residential remodelers reported some or serious shortages of skilled trade subcontractors, according to an NAHB survey cited by JCHS. That is why we weight the budget toward the project types your crews and subcontractors can deliver. Meta can also run hiring as its own campaign with its own budget, kept separate from lead generation.

**Key Takeaway:** Larger and specialty replacement firms should market each product line as its own system, sized to what crews and subcontractors can deliver.

## Tracking Signed Projects

A remodeling buyer may find you through a search, click an ad a week later, and call after a neighbor mentions your name. Capture each lead's source with call tracking and form fields, carry it into your CRM, and report signed projects and revenue by channel instead of lead counts. Our [guide to marketing attribution](/blog/marketing-attribution-home-service-businesses) explains how those pieces fit together.

The right model depends on size. For firms between $1 million and $3 million in revenue, compare first-touch credit with last-touch credit. First-touch credit goes to the channel that introduced the buyer. Last-touch credit goes to the channel just before they booked. The gap shows which channels introduce buyers and which close them. Between $3 million and $10 million, a simple multi-touch model becomes worth the effort.
