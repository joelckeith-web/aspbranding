The difference between SEO, AEO, GEO and PPC is where your business shows up and whether you pay for the spot. SEO earns a place in Google's regular results and the map pack. AEO, short for answer engine optimization, earns a place inside Google's own answers, such as AI Overviews, AI Mode and featured snippets. GEO, short for generative engine optimization, works on how standalone AI assistants like ChatGPT and Perplexity describe your business when someone asks who to call.

PPC, or pay-per-click advertising, is the paid option, and it now reaches into AI answers too. Google says [existing Search, Shopping and Performance Max campaigns are automatically eligible](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/16297775?hl=en) to serve ads within AI Overviews and AI Mode. OpenAI [began testing ads in ChatGPT](https://openai.com/index/testing-ads-in-chatgpt/) in the U.S. on February 9, 2026. For 2027, the split between paid and earned no longer falls between PPC and the other three. It falls inside the AI answer, where sponsored ads and earned mentions now share the same screen.

Where to start still depends on your runway. If you need booked jobs this month, start with paid search and build organic work underneath it. If lead flow is steady but your cost per lead climbs every year, put more weight on SEO. At ASP, a marketing agency for HVAC, plumbing, electrical, roofing and remodeling companies, this is how we explain the choice to owners who ask. The sections below cover what each one is, what changed in 2026, what each one costs, how fast each one works and the order we would take the steps in.

## Do You Need Leads Now or Lower Lead Costs Later?

One of the biggest factors in where to start is how fast you need more leads. Paid ads are the quickest way to reach buyers, because you are buying the placement directly, and the leads stop when the spending does. SEO, AEO and GEO need no ad spend, but they take labor and time. SEO and AEO take months to build, nobody can verify a timeline for GEO, and organic results keep working after they start as long as someone keeps them accurate.

The chart below shows where each one sits on speed and ad spend. It now carries one addition for 2027. Google Search and Performance Max ads can also show inside AI Overviews and AI Mode, so paying for search ads already makes you eligible for some AI answers without a separate campaign.

**Key Takeaway:** If you need booked jobs this month, paid search is still the fastest lever, and those same Google ads are already eligible to appear inside Google's AI answers.

## What Is the Difference Between SEO, AEO and GEO?

[Google's SEO Starter Guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide) says SEO "is about helping search engines understand your content, and helping users find your site and make a decision about whether they should visit your site through a search engine." For a plumber or HVAC company, that means showing up in the map pack, in the organic results and on the service page a homeowner reads before calling. It also covers the technical side, so Google can crawl and index every page you publish. Our [home service SEO page](/seo) lays out how we run that work, and our [HVAC SEO guide](/blog/hvac-seo) applies it to heating and cooling companies.

AEO stands for answer engine optimization. It is the work of earning a place as the source that Google's answer features quote, including AI Overviews, AI Mode, featured snippets and People Also Ask. Google states that [the best practices for SEO remain relevant for AI features](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features) in Search, and that there are no additional requirements to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode. For that reason we treat [answer engine optimization](/aeo) as SEO held to a higher bar, because a page has to be clear and trusted enough for Google to summarize it. Our guide on [how to rank in AI search](/blog/how-to-rank-in-ai-search) walks through that work step by step.

GEO stands for [generative engine optimization](/blog/what-is-generative-engine-optimization). The term comes from a [2023 research paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09735) that introduced GEO as a way to help content creators improve their visibility in generative engine responses. We use it for the chat assistants outside Google's results page, such as ChatGPT, Perplexity and Claude. For a local business, the practical work is keeping your public record accurate, which means your Business Profile, reviews, listings and website. Our post on [whether GEO works](/blog/does-geo-work) covers what the evidence supports so far.

In our experience, people use AEO and GEO loosely and often treat them as one thing. We draw the line by platform, with Google's own answer surfaces under AEO and standalone assistants under GEO. Keeping them apart keeps the evidence for each one separate, because each rests on a different mechanism. Our [GEO page](/geo) explains where that line sits and what nobody can measure yet.

**Key Takeaway:** SEO, AEO and GEO share most of their groundwork, and the main difference is which screen your customer is looking at when your business comes up.

## Where PPC Fits Next to the Other Three

PPC stands for pay-per-click advertising. In Google Ads, [cost-per-click bidding means you pay for each click on your ads](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/116495?hl=en). Local Services Ads charge differently. Google tells advertisers they [pay only for leads related to your business and the services you offer](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/6224841?hl=en), and the Local Services unit is shown prominently in Google Search results when people search for those services in the areas you chose.

For most of the home service operators we work with, PPC means [three channels running together](/blog/ppc-advertising-for-home-service-businesses). Those are Google Search Ads, Local Services Ads and a small Meta retargeting budget. Local Services Ads are now moving into Google Ads as a pay-per-lead version of Performance Max, which we cover further down.

The biggest difference between PPC and the other three is what happens when you stop paying. Your ads stop showing when the budget runs out. Organic pages, answers and listings keep working after you stop adding to them, although they need upkeep to stay accurate.

What has changed is where paid placements can appear. Ads now run inside Google's AI answers, and OpenAI is testing ads inside ChatGPT. PPC is still the only one of the four that buys its placement, but that placement now includes space inside some AI answers. SEO, AEO and GEO are still earned, and in an AI answer the earned mention and the sponsored ad can sit on the same screen.

In our experience, most established businesses end up running SEO and PPC side by side. The search terms that turn into booked jobs in an ad account also show you which organic pages are worth building next. Our [PPC management page](/ppc) walks through how we audit and run those accounts.

**Key Takeaway:** PPC is still the only one of the four that buys placement directly, and the placement it buys now includes space inside some AI answers.

## How Do Ads Show Up in Google AI Overviews and AI Mode?

![Two-by-two table: Google AI answers and ChatGPT, each with an earned spot (citation or link) and a paid spot (sponsored ad), with the rule for each.](/images/blog/seo-vs-ppc-ai-answer-earned-vs-paid.svg)
*Earned mentions and sponsored ads now share the same AI answers on Google and ChatGPT.*

Google does not ask you to build anything new for these placements. Its Google Ads help page says "[Advertisers don't need to create new campaigns or set up separate budgets](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/16297775?hl=en) to show up with their ads in AI Overviews or AI Mode." Existing Search, Shopping and Performance Max campaigns are automatically eligible, and text ads and Shopping ads can appear there. Google says these ads use the same auction and the same cost-per-click or conversion-based bidding models as standard Google Search ads.

You also get less control than some owners expect. Google says, "Just like other features on the Google Search results page, you can't target ads exclusively to AI Overviews or AI Mode, or opt out of showing ads in them separately from Search." These ads are available in English in the United States and select English-speaking markets. So if you run eligible Google Search ads for a U.S. home service business, your ads can already show inside AI answers, and you cannot switch that off without switching off Search. Every ad in these spots is labeled "Sponsored," and Google says "Paid advertising does not influence the organic responses generated by Google AI."

Local Services Ads are a separate case. They are not on Google's list of campaign types eligible for AI Overviews and AI Mode. Google also says the new pay-per-lead campaigns that replace them [will continue to show exclusively on Google Search and Google Maps](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/17213585?hl=en) in the same ad positions as before.

Google plans to put more ads in these answers. On its [October 2026 earnings call](https://s206.q4cdn.com/479360582/files/doc_events/2026/Oct/28/2026_Q3_Earnings_Transcript.pdf), Google said, "Based on these collective results, we are now scaling high-quality ads within AI Overviews and AI Mode to more users and queries." In October 2026, Google also said it is [launching multiturn ads in the U.S.](https://blog.google/products/ads-commerce/ads-in-ai-mode-global/) to allow ads in follow-up queries in AI Mode. In May 2026, Google said it would bring [new ad formats to Search](https://blog.google/products/ads-commerce/google-marketing-live-search-ads/) in the coming months, including a Business Agent for Leads. Google says, "Business Agent for Leads, built with Gemini, puts a smart brand agent right inside your ad." Google has not given a rollout date for home service businesses. Based on these plans, we expect more of the AI answers your customers read in 2027 to carry ads.

What nobody has published yet is home-service results. Neither Google nor OpenAI has released click, cost or lead data for home service ads inside AI answers. Google's only published comparison covers product listings in AI Mode, where it says click-through rates are similar for sponsored and organic items, and it has not released the underlying numbers. That claim tells an HVAC or plumbing owner nothing about calls or booked jobs, so judge these placements by the booked jobs in your own account.

**Key Takeaway:** If you already run Google Search or Performance Max ads, they are already eligible for AI answers, and since neither Google nor OpenAI has published home-service results yet, booked jobs are the number to watch.

## Should a Home Service Business Advertise in ChatGPT?

On January 16, 2026, OpenAI [announced plans to test ads](https://openai.com/index/our-approach-to-advertising-and-expanding-access/) in the U.S. for ChatGPT's Free and Go tiers. It said, "To start, we plan to test ads at the bottom of answers in ChatGPT when there's a relevant sponsored product or service based on your current conversation." OpenAI said Plus, Pro, Business and Enterprise subscriptions would not include ads. It also said "Ads do not influence the answers ChatGPT gives you."

The [test began on February 9, 2026](https://openai.com/index/testing-ads-in-chatgpt/), for logged-in adult users on the Free and Go tiers. OpenAI says it decides which ad to show by matching advertisers' ads with the topic of your conversation, your past chats and past interactions with ads. On May 5, 2026, OpenAI began rolling out a [beta self-serve Ads Manager](https://openai.com/index/new-ways-to-buy-chatgpt-ads/) and added cost-per-click bidding. It said it had recently launched a Conversions API and pixel-based measurement so advertisers can better understand what happens after someone engages with an ad, such as a lead. OpenAI says, "We're gradually opening Ads Manager to more businesses as we continue to test and refine the experience."

The audience is large. OpenAI says [ChatGPT reaches 1.2 billion people each week](https://openai.com/index/new-chatgpt-ads-format-and-measurement/), although that figure covers all ChatGPT use, not searches for local services. The proof for our industry is missing. The advertiser case studies in OpenAI's October 2026 ads post come from e-commerce and wellness brands, and we have found no home-service performance data for ChatGPT ads. OpenAI has not published a cost per click, cost per lead or booking rate for ChatGPT ads in HVAC, plumbing or roofing, and we have not found one elsewhere.

Our recommendation for 2027 is to treat ChatGPT ads as a small test, not a starting point. It fits an owner who already tracks calls and forms back to booked jobs, because that tracking is the only way to tell whether the clicks turn into work. If your tracking cannot do that yet, fix the tracking first and let the channel mature while you do.

**Key Takeaway:** ChatGPT ads are a real but early channel with no home-service results published, so in 2027 we would only test them with a small budget and tracking that ties clicks to booked jobs.

## What Is Changing With Google Local Services Ads?

Local Services Ads are moving into the main Google Ads platform. Google says, "Over the upcoming months, existing Local Services Ads campaigns [will be automatically migrated](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/17213585?hl=en) to a specialized Performance Max campaign type optimized specifically for pay-per-lead goals." The pricing model stays the same. You still pay only for valid leads, such as phone calls and messages, rather than ad clicks.

Google has laid out the move in three phases. In August 2026, the first phase began for select home and storefront service advertisers in the United States, including plumbing, HVAC, electrical, roofing and pest control. In late 2026, the move expands to broader groups, including service-area businesses without physical storefronts and accounts with custom bidding or booking setups. In 2027, Google says non-U.S. accounts and all remaining business categories that weren't migrated in 2026 will transition to Google Ads.

Some things stay the same and some do not. Google says these ads will keep showing only on Google Search and Google Maps, in the same ad positions as before. Manual bidding and industry-level Target CPA are being deprecated, and Google says, "Note that previous performance reports won't carry over to Google Ads." That means the history you use to judge your Local Services Ads today will not follow your account into the new campaign type.

The way you are charged is also spelled out. Google says that with [pay-per-lead goals](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/17325249?hl=en), you're charged only when a potential customer contacts your business directly through your ad, rather than when they click on it. Starting in October 2026, a customer booking an appointment directly through the ad became an optional lead type. Google also says these campaigns use data from your Google Business Profile to set up campaigns optimized for local leads. The Business Profile cleanup we recommend for SEO and GEO is now part of your paid setup as well.

Our recommendation is to download your Local Services Ads lead history and reports before your account moves, so you keep a baseline to compare against. Then check that your Business Profile lists the right services, service area, hours and phone number before Google builds a campaign from it.

**Key Takeaway:** Google says Local Services Ads keep pay-per-lead pricing after they move into Google Ads, but old reports do not carry over, so save your history and clean up your Business Profile before the switch.

## What Each One Costs in Time and Money

We are not going to print price ranges here, because the real number depends on your market, your site and what is already broken. What we can lay out is where the money and the time go for each one. This is how we explain it to owners who ask.

SEO costs mostly labor and your time. In our view, content that earns rankings comes from first-hand experience, so someone has to interview you or a senior tech. Technical fixes and new service pages get built over months.

PPC has two separate costs. Ad spend goes straight to the platform, whether that is Google, Meta or now OpenAI, and management is a separate fee. A sensible budget works back from what a booked job is worth to you. Local Services Ads charge per valid lead instead of per click, and ChatGPT ads can be bought per click through a beta Ads Manager.

At ASP, AEO has no line item of its own, because it runs on the same work as SEO. The added cost is a higher standard of writing and page structure. GEO costs mostly the Business Profile, review and listing work a local SEO program already covers, so for many businesses it adds little new spending.

Be cautious with anyone selling AEO or GEO as a separate package built around special files or markup. Google's guide to its generative AI features says [you don't need to create new machine readable files, AI text files, markup, or Markdown](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide) to appear in Google Search. Our post on [llms.txt for local businesses](/blog/llms-txt-for-local-business) goes deeper on that pitch. For ChatGPT, one real technical check costs almost nothing. OpenAI says [sites that are opted out of OAI-SearchBot will not be shown in ChatGPT search answers](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/bots), though they can still appear as navigational links, so your robots.txt file should allow that crawler.

**Key Takeaway:** PPC costs cash every month on Google, Meta or ChatGPT, SEO costs labor and patience, and AEO and GEO should mostly ride on work you are already paying for.

## How Fast Each One Works

![Line chart of lead flow over months for paid and organic work. The PPC line rises fast, holds while the budget runs, then drops near zero when the budget stops. The organic line, which stands for SEO and AEO, starts slowly, climbs, then levels off and keeps producing leads, labeled "Slow start, keeps returning with upkeep." GEO is not plotted.](/images/blog/seo-vs-ppc-leads-over-time.svg)

*Paid leads stop when the budget stops. Organic work from SEO and AEO starts slowly, then levels off and keeps returning leads as long as it gets upkeep. GEO is left off because no one can verify its timeline.*

PPC is the fastest of the four. Paid search puts you in front of buyers as soon as your ads start running, and it stops when the budget stops. In our experience, an account still needs real lead data before it learns which searches pay. ChatGPT ads can also be bought per click, so their leads would stop when the budget stops, but no one has published how fast they produce home-service leads.

SEO takes longer, and Google says so. Its starter guide notes that [some changes might take effect in a few hours, while others could take several months](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide). Google also says [no one can guarantee a #1 ranking on Google](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/do-i-need-seo). In our experience, technical fixes show first, on-page changes next, and new content clusters move slowest but last longest.

In our view, AEO moves on the same clock as SEO, because Google's AI answers draw their supporting links from the same index. Google says a page [must be indexed and eligible to be shown in Google Search with a snippet](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features) to appear as a supporting link in AI Overviews or AI Mode. Nobody can quote a reliable fixed timeline for it. In our experience, low-competition local questions tend to move before broad category questions do.

GEO is the hardest to time, and the reporting is uneven. On February 10, 2026, Microsoft introduced [AI Performance in Bing Webmaster Tools](https://blogs.bing.com/webmaster/February-2026/Introducing-AI-Performance-in-Bing-Webmaster-Tools-Public-Preview) as a public preview. It shows how often your content is cited in Microsoft Copilot and in AI-generated summaries in Bing, and which of your pages are referenced. It reports citations, Microsoft's announcement describes no impression or click data, and we have not found an equivalent report for ChatGPT. Anyone who promises a date for a ChatGPT mention is promising something no one outside OpenAI controls. In our experience, the work behind GEO also improves your local search presence, so the effort still pays off in search while you wait.

**Key Takeaway:** If you need jobs this month, only PPC moves that fast, while SEO and AEO build over months and GEO has no timeline anyone can verify.

## How AI Search Changed the Decision

In Pew's March 2025 browsing data, people clicked less often when Google showed an AI summary. A [Pew Research Center study](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/) tracked the browsing of 900 U.S. adults in March 2025. Users who saw an AI summary clicked a traditional search result in 8% of visits, compared with 15% for users who did not see one. About 18% of the Google searches in that study produced an AI summary. Treat those figures as a March 2025 snapshot, because we have not found a newer Pew click study.

The newer numbers describe reach rather than clicks. Google said in July 2026 that [AI Mode had surpassed 1 billion monthly active users](https://blog.google/company-news/inside-google/message-ceo/alphabet-earnings-q2-2026/) since it expanded globally the previous October. In a [Pew survey from February 2026](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2026/06/17/americans-and-ai-2026-chatbots-smart-devices-and-views-on-impact/), 60% of U.S. adults said they read AI summaries at the top of search results, and about half reported using AI chatbots. That survey records what people say they do, so it does not replace the 2025 click data.

Fewer clicks do not remove [the need for SEO](/blog/is-seo-dead). Google says [the best practices for SEO continue to be relevant](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide) because its generative AI features are rooted in its core Search ranking and quality systems. In October 2026, Google said it is [building one experience across AI Overviews, AI Mode and traditional Search](https://blog.google/company-news/inside-google/message-ceo/alphabet-earnings-q3-2026/). Google's AI answers draw their supporting links from indexed pages, the same pages that compete for rankings.

What changes for 2027 is what sits around your earned spot. A clear page that answers a homeowner's question well has two chances to be seen, once as a ranking and once as a source inside an answer. That same answer can now also carry a sponsored ad, possibly from a competitor. Because some searches end without a click, calls and booked jobs are a better measure of this work than traffic alone. Our [SEO and AEO predictions for 2027](/blog/seo-aeo-predictions-2027) cover where we expect this to go next year.

**Key Takeaway:** AI answers now carry both earned citations and paid ads, but on Google the earned side is still built on the same index SEO works on, so the fundamentals still decide who gets quoted there.

## How to Decide Where to Start in 2027

The right starting point depends on your situation more than on which term is trending. Find the line below that sounds most like your business right now.

- **You need booked jobs this month.** Start with paid search, and consider Local Services Ads, which charge per lead instead of per click. Build organic work underneath so you are not renting every lead forever.
- **Your lead flow is steady, but cost per lead climbs every year.** Put more weight on SEO. Organic work takes months to move and keeps returning after it does, which is where the margin is.
- **You already rank for your main services.** Move to AEO and tighten those pages. Put the direct answer near the top and the details only your team knows below it.
- **Your Business Profile, reviews or listings are messy.** Fix the public record first. In our experience the same cleanup helps [map-pack rankings](/blog/local-seo-for-home-service-businesses), and it gives AI assistants and Google's pay-per-lead campaigns accurate facts to work from.
- **You want to try ChatGPT ads.** Wait until your tracking ties calls and forms to booked jobs, then run a small test you can measure.
- **Someone is pitching you a separate AI package.** Ask what it does that SEO and local work do not. If it depends on a special file or markup, Google's own guidance says you do not need one to appear in Google Search, including its AI features.

Whatever you choose, [measure it against booked jobs](/blog/marketing-attribution-home-service-businesses) rather than clicks or rankings alone. Over time, AEO and GEO should ride on the same pages, reviews and listings that your SEO work builds.

**Key Takeaway:** Pick your starting point by runway, using paid when you need jobs now, organic when you need margin, and clean listings before anyone sells you an AI shortcut.

## What Should a Home Service Owner Do First?

If you only have time for a few steps before 2027, take them in this order:

1. **Set up tracking that ties calls and forms to booked jobs.** Every other decision on this page depends on knowing which channel produced the work.
2. **Clean up your Google Business Profile, reviews and listings.** In our experience, that record feeds your local rankings and the facts AI assistants read, and Google says its pay-per-lead campaigns are set up from your Business Profile data.
3. **Save your Local Services Ads history if you run them.** Download your lead history and reports before your account moves into Google Ads, because old reports won't carry over.
4. **Check that your robots.txt file does not block OAI-SearchBot.** OpenAI says sites that block it will not be shown in ChatGPT search answers, though they can still appear as navigational links.
5. **Run paid search if you need jobs now, and judge it by booked jobs.** Remember that those Google ads can also show inside AI Overviews and AI Mode.
6. **Build service pages that answer homeowners' questions clearly.** The same pages serve your rankings and your chances of being quoted in an AI answer.
7. **Hold ChatGPT ads until step 1 works.** Then test with a small budget and compare the booked jobs against your other channels.
