Yes, there are proven strategies for ranking in AI search, but they are more basic than most sales pitches suggest. Google, OpenAI, Perplexity and Microsoft have each published what their AI search features need from a website. Almost all of it is [standard SEO](/seo): let the crawlers in, get your pages indexed, keep your business information current, and publish useful pages built from real experience. Going into 2027, most other advice on how to rank in AI search is still speculation, even when it sounds technical.

Ranking in AI search means getting your business named, quoted or linked inside an AI-generated answer. The audience for those answers is now very large. [Google says](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/search/new-opportunities-control-and-insights-for-website-owners/) "AI Overviews now has over 2.5 billion monthly active users, while AI Mode has surpassed one billion monthly users." [OpenAI says](https://openai.com/index/introducing-businesses-chatgpt/) 1.2 billion people use ChatGPT each week.

At ASP, a marketing agency for HVAC, plumbing, roofing, remodeling and other home service businesses, we split this work by platform. [Answer engine optimization](/aeo) (AEO) covers Google's AI Overviews and AI Mode, and [generative engine optimization](/geo) (GEO) covers standalone assistants like ChatGPT and Perplexity. Our [comparison of SEO, PPC, AEO and GEO](/blog/seo-vs-ppc-vs-aeo-vs-geo) shows how they fit together. This post separates what the platforms have documented from what nobody has proven, and it covers the late-2026 changes we would plan around for 2027.

## What Has Google Documented About Its AI Features?

Of the four platforms, Google has published the most about its AI search features, and its message has stayed consistent. Google's [guide to optimizing for generative AI features on Google Search](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide) gives the reason. It says, "The best practices for SEO continue to be relevant because our generative AI features on Google Search are rooted in our core Search ranking and quality systems." The same guide also says, "From Google Search's perspective, optimizing for generative AI search is optimizing for the search experience, and thus still SEO."

The entry requirement is written down too. Google's [AI features documentation](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features) says that to be a supporting link in AI Overviews or AI Mode, a page "must be indexed and eligible to be shown in Google Search with a snippet." That page also says there are no additional requirements and no other special optimizations necessary. Google's optimization guide adds one condition: a site "must be included in Search generative AI features in Search Console to be eligible for display in generative AI features on Google Search."

Inclusion is the default, so most sites already meet that condition, and the next section shows how to check yours. Google also explains why one page can appear for a question it never targeted. The [AI features documentation](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features) says both features may use a "query fan-out" technique, which the optimization guide describes as "A set of concurrent, related queries generated by the model to request more information and fetch additional relevant search results to address the user's query."

For example, a homeowner who asks why their furnace keeps shutting off could set off related searches about filters, flame sensors and repair costs. A heating company with solid pages on those topics gives Google more to draw from than one generic furnace repair page does. Those pages should exist because customers ask those questions, and a later section covers what Google says about building pages only to catch searches.

**Key Takeaway:** Google's AI answers come from its normal index, so a page that cannot show in regular search with a snippet, or a site set to exclude, cannot be cited.

## How to Check That Your Site Is Included in Google's AI Features

In 2026, Google gave site owners a switch that controls whether their site appears in its AI features. Google's [help page on the Search generative AI control](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/16908024?hl=en) says, "As of August 31, 2026, we've rolled out this control to all websites worldwide." The control covers AI Overviews, AI Mode and generative AI features in Google Discover. You can find it in Search Console under Settings > Search generative AI.

The option to "Include my site's links and content" is the default, and Google says, "This is the default control for all properties." If a site is set to exclude, Google says, "You won't receive any traffic or impressions from these features." Google adds that the control isn't used as a ranking or inclusion signal for other parts of Search. It also says the control doesn't affect AI training, and it points site owners to Google-Extended for that.

For most home service companies, nothing needs to change. We would still check the setting once, especially if a past web developer or marketing vendor had admin access to your Search Console account. An Exclude setting chosen by someone else would keep your site out of AI Overviews and AI Mode.

Even with the setting correct, Google does not promise a spot. Its [optimization guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide) says, "Just because a page meets all requirements, best practices, and complies with the policies, doesn't mean that Google will crawl, index, or serve its content."

**Key Takeaway:** Open Search Console, go to Settings > Search generative AI, and confirm your site is set to Include before you spend money on any other AI search work.

## What Do ChatGPT, Perplexity and Copilot Say?

The standalone assistants publish far less, but what they do publish is useful. OpenAI's [crawler documentation](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/bots) says "OAI-SearchBot is used to surface websites in search results in ChatGPT's search features." OpenAI recommends allowing that crawler in your robots.txt file and allowing requests from its published IP ranges. It also says, "Sites that are opted out of OAI-SearchBot will not be shown in ChatGPT search answers, though can still appear as navigational links."

OpenAI says it can take about 24 hours after a robots.txt update for its systems to adjust. It also treats each of its crawlers separately. Its page says a site can allow OAI-SearchBot so it appears in search results while blocking GPTBot, which signals that crawled content should not be used to train OpenAI's models.

Perplexity's [bot documentation](https://docs.perplexity.ai/guides/bots) gives the same advice for PerplexityBot, which it says "is designed to surface and link websites in search results on Perplexity." The page says, "To ensure your site appears in search results, we recommend allowing PerplexityBot in your site's robots.txt file and permitting requests from our published IP ranges listed below."

Microsoft ties its assistant to its existing search engine. On its [advertising blog](https://about.ads.microsoft.com/en/blog/post/october-2025/optimizing-your-content-for-inclusion-in-ai-search-answers), Microsoft wrote that experiences like Microsoft Copilot are "powered by Bing's search index." Microsoft's [February 2026 Bing Webmaster Tools post](https://blogs.bing.com/webmaster/February-2026/Introducing-AI-Performance-in-Bing-Webmaster-Tools-Public-Preview) adds advice for local businesses. It says businesses can register with Bing Places for Business so key details like address, hours and contact information stay "current and eligible for inclusion in AI-generated responses."

That same post describes IndexNow as a way of "notifying participating search engines whenever content is added, updated, or removed." The platforms do not agree on every detail, though. Microsoft's October 2025 post says assistants like Copilot break content into "smaller, structured pieces" and describes schema as code that "helps search engines and AI systems understand your content." Google says neither is required for its own features.

None of these companies publish a list of the factors that decide which business gets recommended. OpenAI's and Perplexity's crawler pages cover access, not ranking.

**Key Takeaway:** For ChatGPT and Perplexity, the only published requirement is letting their search crawlers read your site, and for Copilot the starting point is Bing's index and an accurate Bing Places listing.

## Should You Claim Your Business in ChatGPT?

OpenAI added a new option for businesses in late 2026. On November 10, 2026, it [introduced Businesses in ChatGPT](https://openai.com/index/introducing-businesses-chatgpt/) in beta. OpenAI says, "Through Business Hub, businesses can claim their business, manage their information, and see insights on how people engage with them on ChatGPT." According to [OpenAI's Businesses page](https://openai.com/business/businesses-in-chatgpt), a blue check appears on a business profile in ChatGPT once it has been claimed.

Eligibility is still an open question for home service companies. OpenAI says, "Eligibility depends on factors including industry, location, and our verification and integrity checks." It also says, "Claiming is initially available to eligible businesses with a website." OpenAI's pages do not spell out whether a service-area business like an HVAC or plumbing company qualifies today, so check Business Hub for your own company.

Set your expectations before you claim. OpenAI says, "ChatGPT continues to make independent recommendations and comparisons." It also says, "Claiming does not guarantee higher placement or sales." We treat a ChatGPT listing the way we treat a Google Business Profile: it keeps your information accurate and can give you some data once there is enough activity, but it does not buy a spot in the answer.

If you can claim it, do it in an account your business owns, not an employee's personal login or a vendor's account. Our [SEO and AEO predictions for 2027](/blog/seo-aeo-predictions-2027) explain why we expect ChatGPT to become a bigger source of business data and why account ownership comes first.

**Key Takeaway:** If your business is eligible, claim it in ChatGPT's Business Hub under a company-owned account, and treat it as a way to keep your details accurate, not as a ranking boost.

## What Google Says Does Not Help

Google's guide spends a surprising amount of space on tactics it says you can skip. On llms.txt and similar files, the [guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide) says you don't need to create "new machine readable files, AI text files, markup, or Markdown" to appear in Google Search. It gives the reason that "Google Search itself doesn't use them." It adds, "Doing so will neither harm nor help your site's visibility or rankings in Google Search, as Google Search ignores them."

Our post on [llms.txt for local businesses](/blog/llms-txt-for-local-business) covers that file in more detail. On structured data, Google says, "Structured data isn't required for generative AI search, and there's no special schema.org markup you need to add." Its next sentence says structured data is still a good idea as part of your overall SEO strategy, because it helps with being eligible for rich results.

Google also says there is no requirement to break your content into tiny pieces for AI, and that "You don't need to write in a specific way just for generative AI search." On manufactured mentions, the guide says, "However, seeking inauthentic 'mentions' across the web isn't as helpful as it might seem."

The guide also warns against page sprawl. It says creating separate pages for every way people might search, including fan-out queries, "primarily to manipulate rankings or generative AI responses in Google Search violates Google's scaled content abuse spam policy." Google adds, "This is also an ineffective long-term strategy, as a high quantity of pages doesn't make a website higher quality or more relevant to users." We read that as a warning against [bulk AI-written pages produced only to catch searches](/blog/real-seo-vs-fake-seo).

For those reasons, ASP does not sell an llms.txt file or schema as a way into AI answers. Schema still has a real job, which is making a page eligible for rich results, and we build it for that reason. We won't bill it as an AI lever on Google when Google says it isn't required for its AI features.

**Key Takeaway:** Before paying for an AI file, AI schema, an AI writing style or a batch of AI-written pages, check it against Google's own list of things it says you do not need or should not do.

## Where Does the Evidence Run Out?

A [2023 research paper on generative engine optimization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09735), later accepted to the KDD 2024 conference, tested content changes against a research benchmark. Its authors wrote, "Through rigorous evaluation, we demonstrate that GEO can boost visibility by up to 40% in generative engine responses." The abstract also says, "Moreover, we show the efficacy of these strategies varies across domains, underscoring the need for domain-specific optimization methods."

The abstract describes that benchmark as "a large-scale benchmark of diverse user queries across multiple domains." A benchmark from 2023 cannot tell you what ChatGPT says today when a homeowner asks for a plumber in your town. We walk through the published studies in more depth in our review of [whether GEO works and what the evidence shows](/blog/does-geo-work).

Newer research points the same way. A [September 2026 preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16304), which has not been peer reviewed, studied brand recommendations from six large language models. Its authors wrote, "LLMs can generate recommendations without an explicit candidate set, and repeated responses to the same query can produce different brands and rankings." They also found that "prominence is associated with broader marketplace-visibility signals, particularly search interest and online brand conversation."

That study covered five product categories, not local services. Keep those limits in mind when someone promises your business a spot inside ChatGPT or a set number of AI citations. Nobody outside these companies controls what an assistant says, so the person making that promise cannot keep it. Ask the seller which published source supports each step of the plan, and see how many steps hold up.

**Key Takeaway:** Research shows content changes can shift AI visibility on a research benchmark, but no one has published a reliable method for getting a local business recommended.

## Why Do AI Answers Name Different Businesses Each Time?

If you ask an AI tool the same local question twice, you may get a different list of companies. [BrightLocal's local AI visibility study](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-ai-visibility-study/), published September 16, 2026, measured how often that happens. BrightLocal, which sells an AI visibility tracker, reviewed 200,085 localized searches across ChatGPT, Google AI Mode and Google AI Overviews. It tracked 1,300 business locations with non-branded local searches repeated over 60 days.

Repeat searches shared only 23% of their results on average for ChatGPT, 21% for AI Mode and 33% for AI Overviews. A business that appeared once showed up again in about half of later attempts: 50% of the time for ChatGPT and AI Mode, and 58% for AI Overviews. The platforms also disagreed with each other. AI Mode and AI Overviews shared only 29% of businesses, and ChatGPT overlapped just 19% to 20% with either Google surface.

Google Maps named tracked businesses more often. BrightLocal found that Google Maps mentioned a tracked business in 66% of searches, compared with 32% to 38% for the AI surfaces. Storefront locations averaged a 33.6% mention rate, versus 29.4% for service-area businesses, a gap worth watching if your company has no storefront. BrightLocal does not list the industries it studied, and its example searches include an emergency dentist, a family lawyer and a pizza place, so it is not a home service benchmark.

![Bar chart: Google Maps named a tracked business in 66% of searches versus 32-38% for AI surfaces; repeat AI searches overlapped only 21-33%. BrightLocal, Sep 2026.](/images/blog/how-to-rank-in-ai-search-brightlocal-local-mention-rates.svg)

*Google Maps named a tracked business far more often than any AI surface, and repeat AI searches shared only a fraction of their results. Source: BrightLocal, September 2026, a vendor study, not specific to home services.*

For an owner, one screenshot of an AI answer proves very little. A single check can make your business look invisible, or it can make a vendor's work look better than it is. A repeated check, with the same questions asked on a schedule, gives you a fairer picture.

**Key Takeaway:** AI answers change from one search to the next, so judge your visibility on repeated checks over weeks, not on a single screenshot.

## Evidence-Backed Ways to Rank in AI Search

Most of what follows comes from guidance the platforms have published. None of it is exotic, and most of it also helps your regular search results, which is a good sign the money is well spent.

- **Let the right crawlers in.** Check your robots.txt file for blocks on OAI-SearchBot and PerplexityBot. Copilot is powered by Bing's index, so make sure Bing can crawl your site too.
- **Confirm your pages are indexed and your site is included.** In Google Search Console, check that your main service pages are indexed and allowed to show a snippet. Then confirm that Settings > Search generative AI is set to Include.
- **Keep your Google Business Profile and Bing Places listing current.** Google's [guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide) says Business Profiles "can help your products and services to be visible in both AI responses and other Google Search results." Keep your address, hours and [contact information](/blog/why-consistent-nap-info-is-crucial-for-local-seo-success) current on both listings.
- **Write from the job.** Google tells site owners to "Create the content yourself based on what you know about the topic, and consider what in-depth experience you can bring to your content." It warns that commodity content "typically adds little unique insight for readers."
- **Answer the real questions customers ask.** Write about the questions your team hears on the phone, but don't spin out a separate page for every wording of the same question. Google says doing that mainly to manipulate rankings or AI answers violates its scaled content abuse spam policy.
- **Link related pages together.** Google's [AI features documentation](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features) lists "Making your content easily findable through internal links on your website" among the SEO basics that still apply.
- **Claim your ChatGPT listing if you are eligible.** Check Business Hub, and claim the listing under an account your business owns.

Vendor studies also suggest your own website still carries weight. BrightLocal wrote, "Your website still matters." It found that 93% of the unique domains used as sources were business sites, making up 42% of all citations. Google Business Profile made up 28.5% of citations across the three platforms it studied.

[Yext's research](https://www.yext.com/research/articles/ai-citation-behavior-across-models-consistent-source-preferences-across-a-growing-ai-landscape) looked at 155.5 million AI citations across 1,623 brands from January through March 2026. It found that 80% of citations across four AI models pointed at a website or a listing. Yext calls its own findings "hypotheses rather than settled conclusions," and both BrightLocal and Yext sell tools in this space.

For an HVAC or plumbing company, writing from the job means including details a competitor cannot copy from another website. That includes what a repair costs in your market, which failures show up each season, and what you tell a homeowner before they ask. Our [HVAC SEO guide](/blog/hvac-seo) covers the rest of the search work for heating and cooling companies.

**Key Takeaway:** The documented moves for AI search are crawl access, indexing with Google's AI setting on Include, accurate profiles and first-hand content, and each one also helps your regular rankings.

## What Happens When Google Calls Your Business for a Quote?

Google can also contact businesses on a customer's behalf. At Google I/O on May 19, 2026, [Google announced](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/search/search-io-2026/) that "for select categories like home repair, beauty or pet care, you can ask Google to call businesses on your behalf." Google's [help page on checking local pricing with AI](https://support.google.com/websearch/answer/16421135?hl=en) says, "Google will call multiple service providers near you." It says the feature is available in English to signed-in users over 18 in the US, on both mobile and desktop.

Google's [Business Profile help page on automated calls](https://support.google.com/business/answer/16190256?hl=en) explains the business side. Google says it "might contact your business on their behalf through an automated phone call to complete the request." It also says, "If you share this info with Google, the customer receives an email or SMS text that includes your business's product details or service quote." Google says the feature is available in all US states except Indiana, Louisiana, Minnesota, Montana and Nebraska.

When we checked in November 2026, Google's own pages did not confirm that the home repair version is live yet, so treat it as announced rather than available. If it reaches home repair, we expect businesses that answer these calls and give a clear price range to get more out of it than businesses that let calls go to voicemail. That is our expectation, not something Google has said. For now, [keep your Business Profile accurate](/blog/local-seo-for-home-service-businesses) and make sure whoever answers your phone knows how to handle an automated call asking for a quote.

**Key Takeaway:** Google has announced AI calls to home repair businesses on behalf of customers, so make sure your phones get answered and your team can give a price range when one of those calls comes in.

## How Do You Measure AI Search Visibility in 2027?

Measurement is where many AI search services get vague, so start with the reports the platforms provide. We expect 2027 to be the first full year in which Google, Bing and ChatGPT each give business owners some first-party AI data. Each report has limits, and we found nothing comparable from Perplexity.

Google Search Console has a [Generative AI performance report](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/16984139) covering AI Overviews and AI Mode. Google defines its impressions as "how many times links to your site were shown to a user in a generative AI feature on Google Search." Google says it rolled the report out to all websites worldwide as of August 31, 2026. Google's [June 2026 announcement](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/search/new-opportunities-control-and-insights-for-website-owners/) says the insights include "impressions metrics and information about which pages appear in AI responses and in what countries."

Google documents impressions for this report, not clicks, and it says "we'll introduce additional metrics over time." Microsoft's Bing Webmaster Tools has an [AI Performance report](https://blogs.bing.com/webmaster/February-2026/Introducing-AI-Performance-in-Bing-Webmaster-Tools-Public-Preview), announced as a public preview in February 2026. Microsoft says it "shows how publisher content appears across Microsoft Copilot, AI-generated summaries in Bing, and select partner integrations."

In [June 2026](https://blogs.bing.com/search/June-2026/New-AI-Visibility-Insights-in-Bing-Webmaster-Tools-Intents-Topics-Citation-Share-Compare), Bing added Intents, Topics, Citation Share and Compare, and its intent categories include "Local." Microsoft describes Citation Share as "an observational metric – not a ranking system or a competitive scoreboard." In [October 2026](https://blogs.bing.com/webmaster/October-2026/Introducing-Topics-Portfolio-Beta-in-AI-Performance), Bing added a Topics Portfolio beta with "Top Opportunity Queries" where other sites appear and yours does not. Microsoft says to "Treat the numbers as directional signals for planning, not as a precise ledger," and smaller sites "may see a shorter list or may not even see this feature."

For ChatGPT, [OpenAI's Business Hub](https://openai.com/index/introducing-businesses-chatgpt/) gives claimed businesses "the number of mentions and website clicks a business receives." It also shows discovery topics, which OpenAI describes as "common themes across conversations where their website is cited." OpenAI says the insights "are only available when there are enough conversations or interactions to meet certain thresholds," so we expect many smaller local companies to see little data at first. When we checked in November 2026, we found no comparable first-party business report from Perplexity.

![Table of first-party AI visibility reports: Google Search Console, Bing Webmaster Tools and ChatGPT Business Hub, each with limits, and no Perplexity report found.](/images/blog/how-to-rank-in-ai-search-ai-visibility-reports-2027.svg)

*Google, Bing and ChatGPT now offer first-party AI reports, each with limits. We found no comparable Perplexity report, so a written spot-check log is the record there.*

Google's [optimization guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide) also warns, "Be wary of third-party tools that promise ranking success or claim to use 'internal' Google metrics." It adds, "No third-party tool has access to our internal ranking or AI systems." On every platform, a practical outside check is a repeatable spot-check: a fixed set of homeowner-style questions, asked in the same places on a schedule, with the results written down.

The September 2026 preprint supports that method. Its authors say recommendations should be evaluated as "a stochastic retrieval-and-ranking process rather than from individual generated lists," which means a process with built-in random variation. After that, tie any change back to calls, leads and booked jobs. Impressions and mentions are early signals, and booked jobs are the result you are paying for.

**Key Takeaway:** Use Search Console, Bing Webmaster Tools and Business Hub where you qualify, keep a written spot-check log for the rest, and judge all of it against calls and booked jobs.

## What Should a Home Service Owner Do First?

If you only have time for a few steps before 2027, do them in this order:

1. **Check Google's AI setting.** In Search Console, open Settings > Search generative AI and confirm your site is set to Include.
2. **Check crawler access.** Make sure your robots.txt file allows OAI-SearchBot and PerplexityBot, and that Google and Bing can crawl your site.
3. **Fix your profiles.** Update your Google Business Profile and Bing Places listing so your hours, services, service area and contact details are accurate and current.
4. **Claim your ChatGPT listing if you are eligible.** Check Business Hub, and claim the listing under an account your business owns.
5. **Start a spot-check log.** Pick about 10 questions homeowners ask about your trade and town, run them monthly in Google, ChatGPT, Copilot and Perplexity, and write down which companies get named.
6. **Improve your service pages from the job.** Add the costs, seasonal problems and advice only your team can give, and skip bulk AI-written pages.
7. **Track calls and booked jobs by source.** Judge AI search work on the jobs it produces, not on impressions alone.

None of this comes with a guarantee.
