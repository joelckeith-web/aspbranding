The HVAC marketing strategies that work best follow the heating and cooling seasons and judge every channel by the jobs it books. That means Local Services Ads and search ads for urgent repair calls, plus a strong Business Profile and service pages for planned work. It also means tracking that shows which calls turned into revenue.

This guide walks through each piece for an established heating and cooling company that wants steadier HVAC lead generation through the year. It draws on what Google, federal agencies and ENERGY STAR have published, one dated industry cost guide, and how ASP runs [HVAC marketing](/industries/hvac) for companies whose busiest months depend on the weather.

## What Is HVAC Marketing?

HVAC marketing is the work a heating and cooling company does to get found, chosen and called by homeowners who need repair, maintenance or a new system. It covers search ads, Local Services Ads, your Google Business Profile, your website, reviews, past-customer follow-up and local offline ads, all measured by the jobs they book.

For an established company, that work has two sides. One side makes sure a homeowner with a broken system finds you and calls. The other keeps you in front of homeowners who already know you, so their next tune-up, repair or replacement comes back to your company. The next section explains how seasonal demand shapes the year. After that, nine numbered strategies cover both sides, followed by a budget guide and an example of how the year can be laid out.

## Seasonal Demand in Heating and Cooling

Heating and cooling work rises and falls with the weather. The U.S. Energy Information Administration [reports](https://www.eia.gov/energyexplained/use-of-energy/homes.php) that space heating and air conditioning made up 52 percent of the average household's energy use in 2020, and it describes both uses as mostly seasonal. The Bureau of Labor Statistics [notes](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/installation-maintenance-and-repair/heating-air-conditioning-and-refrigeration-mechanics-and-installers.htm) that HVAC technicians may be on call for emergencies and sometimes work overtime or irregular schedules during peak heating and cooling seasons.

That leaves an HVAC company with two kinds of demand to plan for. The first is the urgent repair call during a heat wave or a cold snap, when the homeowner wants a phone number and a quick answer. The second is planned work, like tune-ups and system replacements, which a homeowner can research, compare and schedule.

ENERGY STAR's [maintenance checklist](https://www.energystar.gov/saveathome/heating-cooling/maintenance-checklist) tells homeowners to have the cooling system checked in the spring and the heating system in the fall. The reason it gives is that contractors get busy once summer and winter come. Those two windows are the natural home for maintenance marketing.

**Key Takeaway:** Market to two kinds of demand. Urgent repair calls cluster in the peaks, while tune-ups belong in the spring and fall windows ENERGY STAR points homeowners toward.

## 1. Local Services Ads and Google Search Ads

A homeowner whose system just quit wants a company they can call right away. Paid search puts your number at the top of the page. Google offers two ways to buy that spot.

Local Services Ads are Google's pay-per-lead ads. Google [lists](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/6224841?hl=en) "HVAC (heating or air conditioning)" among its US categories, though availability depends on your area. You pay for leads related to your business rather than for clicks. Each lead arrives as a phone call, a message request or a booking made through the ad. Google treats HVAC as an urgent category because people often call about time-sensitive problems like a broken heater in freezing weather. It also screens every business before ads run.

Your bid is only part of where the ad shows. Google's [page on ad rankings](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/7527305?hl=en) also weighs how likely your ad is to bring a lead and your profile quality. Profile quality covers your rating, number of reviews, response time, photos and completed verification checks. The same page warns that missed calls may hurt your responsiveness. Reviews should come from real customers and stay inside Google's review policies and the FTC's rule on fake reviews. Never offer incentives, set quotas or script what customers say. Google now calls its badge Google Verified, and Google says it will discontinue the money-back guarantee tied to the older badge, so your ads and site should not promise one.

Google Search Ads let you pick which services and areas to push. Repair, replacement and maintenance searches come from different buyers, so each one gets its own ad group. That split shows whether replacement clicks or repair clicks are paying for themselves. Our [HVAC advertising page](/ppc/hvac) covers the account setup in more detail.

**Key Takeaway:** Local Services Ads rank on reviews and responsiveness as well as bid, so an answered phone matters alongside the budget.

## 2. Local SEO and Service Pages

Paid ads go quiet when the budget runs out. Organic visibility takes months to move but keeps returning after it does, and most established operators run both.

The map pack starts with your Google Business Profile. Google's [Business Profile guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177?hl=en) say a service-area business should keep one profile for its central office with a designated service area. A virtual office can't be listed unless it is staffed during business hours, so the other towns you cover get service-area pages on your website instead of extra profiles. Our [local SEO guide for home service businesses](/blog/local-seo-for-home-service-businesses) covers the profile in depth, and [Local SEO Pro](/local-seo-pro) is the service that manages it, including a monthly ranking heatmap.

Your website carries the planned work. Choose which services get their own pages by search demand, what a booked job is worth to you and how strong the competing pages are. BLS projects HVAC mechanic and installer jobs to grow 11 percent from 2025 to 2035, partly because technicians will be needed to retrofit, upgrade or replace existing systems. Heat pumps can earn their own pages, depending on your market. EIA counted about 13 percent of US households heating with a heat pump as their main equipment in 2020, and the same units cool the house as well.

Fill those pages with what only your shop knows. That includes how your techs tell a simple repair from a failing system. It also includes what drives the price of an install in your market and what gets checked first on a service call. Those details come from interviews with you or a senior tech, and your license and credentials belong on the page beside them. Our [HVAC SEO services](/seo/hvac) page and the guide to [what ranks in HVAC SEO](/blog/hvac-seo) go further on the organic side.

**Key Takeaway:** Keep one Business Profile inside Google's rules, and give each high-value service a page built from details only your techs know.

## 3. How to Get More Google Reviews After Every Job

Reviews feed two of the channels above. Google's ranking page for Local Services Ads counts your rating and number of reviews as part of profile quality. Reviews also sit on your Business Profile, where a homeowner comparing companies in the map pack sees them alongside your categories, services and photos.

Make the review request a standard step at the end of every job, so it doesn't depend on someone in the office remembering. Send it soon after the work is done, while the visit is fresh, by text or email with a direct link to your Google review page. Ask every customer the same way, and answer the reviews that come in, including the critical ones, with a short reply a future customer can read.

A review tool keeps that routine running without anyone having to remember it. NiceJob is the review tool ASP prefers for this. Review capture automation is also part of [Local SEO Pro](/local-seo-pro), along with Business Profile management and a monthly ranking report.

Keep every request inside the rules covered in the Local Services Ads section. Reviews should come from real customers, and you should never offer incentives, set quotas or script what customers say. Your Business Profile and its reviews belong to your business, so keep yourself as the profile's primary owner. ASP works on Google reviews first, then Yelp, then the trade directories that show up as sources for your trade, as our post on [Neil Patel's AI SEO rules](/blog/neil-patel-ai-seo-rules) explains.

**Key Takeaway:** Make a review request part of closing every job, use a tool so it goes out every time, and work on Google reviews first, then Yelp.

## 4. Turning HVAC Website Visits Into Calls

Ads, local SEO and reviews all send homeowners to the same two places, your website and your Business Profile. A visit only pays off when it turns into a call, a form or a booking, so the site has to make that step easy.

Urgent calls often start with a phone search, so build every page for a homeowner holding a phone. Put a tap-to-call number at the top, and say in the first lines what you fix and which towns you cover. Offer a short form or online booking for people who can't call right away.

Put your license, credentials and real reviews close to where the homeowner decides to call. Send each ad to the page for the service it advertises, so a homeowner who clicked an ad for furnace replacement lands on a furnace replacement page instead of your home page. Our guide to [websites that convert for home service businesses](/blog/websites-that-convert-for-home-service-businesses) goes through the layout in more detail.

A call only becomes a job if someone answers it, and the company that answers first often books the job. Every missed call is a lead your marketing already paid to bring in. Our [guide to speed to lead for home service businesses](/blog/speed-to-lead-home-service-businesses) covers how to answer faster, including on nights and weekends. On the sites ASP runs, call tracking ties each call to the channel that produced it, so you can see which campaigns are sending callers.

**Key Takeaway:** Build every page for a homeowner on a phone, send each ad to the page for its service, and answer fast so the visits you paid for turn into booked calls.

## 5. Seasonal Content and Weather Spikes

Content has to be live before demand arrives. Cooling and AC maintenance pages should be published and indexed before the spring check-up window, and heating pages should be ready before fall.

Paid budgets should follow the same curve. During heat waves and cold snaps, emergency repair searches and Local Services Ads take the bigger share. In the shoulder months, tune-up and replacement campaigns take the lead. If a soft year has you rethinking spend, our piece on [soft HVAC demand and your marketing budget](/blog/hvac-demand-marketing-budget-2026) walks through that decision.

**Key Takeaway:** Build each season's pages a season early, and move paid budget toward emergency repair terms when the weather turns.

## 6. Meta Ads for Retargeting and Hiring

Meta won't reliably reach a homeowner in the middle of a no-heat emergency, because that person is searching, not scrolling. Its job in HVAC is to keep your name in front of people who read your replacement or heat pump page and left without calling. When they are ready to buy, you are the company they already know.

Meta also helps with hiring. BLS projects about 40,600 openings a year, on average, for HVAC mechanics and installers over the 2025 to 2035 decade. The technicians you want are usually employed and not browsing job boards, and Meta can still reach them. Run recruiting on its own budget, kept apart from lead generation, so hiring spend is never read as lead-generation performance.

**Key Takeaway:** Use Meta to bring back replacement shoppers and to reach working technicians, not to chase emergency calls.

## 7. Marketing to Past HVAC Customers

Homeowners you have already served know your company and have had your techs in their home. Staying in touch gives them a reason to call you first when the next tune-up, repair or replacement comes up.

Maintenance reminders are the simplest place to start. ENERGY STAR's [maintenance checklist](https://www.energystar.gov/saveathome/heating-cooling/maintenance-checklist) tells homeowners to check the cooling system in the spring and the heating system in the fall, and it suggests planning those check-ups around the spring and fall time changes. It also tells them to inspect, clean or change air filters once a month on a central air conditioner, furnace or heat pump. Each of those gives you a natural reason to send a reminder.

Watson & Co. Marketing's April 21, 2026 guide, How Much Should HVAC Companies Spend on Advertising?, lists annual maintenance reminders, seasonal tune-up offers and filter replacement notifications as email that keeps a company top of mind. The same guide suggests running email campaigns to maintenance agreement customers in the fall. If you sell maintenance agreements, those members are the first list to contact before each check-up window.

Follow up on unsold estimates, too. Keep every quote that didn't book in your CRM with the date and the system quoted, and set a reminder to check in with that homeowner. A short, friendly call or email asking whether they have questions is enough.

ASP offers email campaign options in its custom packages. The reminder timing and estimate follow-up described here are general practice that any HVAC company can run with its own team.

**Key Takeaway:** Use the spring and fall check-up windows and monthly filter reminders as reasons to reach past customers, and follow up on every unsold estimate.

## 8. Truck Wraps, Yard Signs and Direct Mail for HVAC Companies

Some local marketing still happens away from a screen. A wrapped truck in a driveway, a yard sign after an install and a postcard before tune-up season all put your name in front of homeowners in the neighborhoods you already serve. Direct mail can also reach past customers ahead of the spring and fall check-up windows.

The hard part of offline marketing is measuring it. Give each piece its own call-tracking number, such as one for the trucks, one for yard signs and one for each mail drop, so calls to that number are counted to that piece. You can then compare it with your other channels on cost per booked job. ASP measures truck wraps, yard signs and direct mail for clients with call-tracking numbers.

Call tracking won't catch every call. A homeowner who sees your truck and then searches your company name won't dial the tracking number, and that call shows up under search instead. Treat the tracked count as the calls you can confirm rather than the full total.

**Key Takeaway:** Once each truck wrap, sign and mail drop has its own call-tracking number, it can be judged on cost per booked job like any other channel.

## 9. Tracking Booked Jobs

A call count says little on its own. A tune-up call and a full system replacement can come from the same campaign and be worth very different amounts. Tag each call and form with its source, carry that source onto the job in your CRM where it supports attribution, and compare channels on cost per booked job. Our guide to [marketing attribution for home service businesses](/blog/marketing-attribution-home-service-businesses) shows how call tracking, forms and the ad platforms connect.

Tracking also shows where calls are slipping. When techs are working overtime in peak season, someone still has to answer the phone. Google's ranking page notes that message and booking leads can help on nights and weekends when you can't pick up. Our [speed to lead guide](/blog/speed-to-lead-home-service-businesses) covers call coverage in more depth.

Ownership belongs in the same conversation. Your ad accounts, Business Profile, website and call tracking data should sit in your name, with ad spend paid straight to the platforms. Our [What You Own page](/what-you-own) lists each asset. With ASP, organic SEO is included from Foundation up, and paid media management is included from Growth up.

**Key Takeaway:** Judge every channel by cost per booked job, and make sure the accounts and data behind that number belong to you.

## Sample HVAC Marketing Plan by Season

The table below shows one way an HVAC company could lay out its year. It is an example, not a plan for any one company, and your months will shift with your climate and services. The season months follow the Watson & Co. Marketing guide cited above, and the check-up windows follow ENERGY STAR.

| Season (example) | What homeowners are calling about | Example marketing focus |
|---|---|---|
| Spring (March–May) | Cooling check-ups. ENERGY STAR points homeowners to a spring cooling check, and the Watson guide says AC tune-up searches spike. | AC tune-up pages already live, maintenance reminders by email and mail, maintenance agreement signups, Meta retargeting for replacement shoppers |
| Summer (June–August) | Emergency AC repair and system replacements, which the Watson guide names as the peak for call volume and replacement consultations | A bigger share of paid budget on emergency repair terms and Local Services Ads, phone coverage for missed calls, a review request after every job |
| Fall (September–November) | Heating check-ups, furnace tune-ups and furnace replacement quotes | Heating pages live before fall, ad keywords shifted from cooling to heating, email to maintenance agreement customers, unsold-estimate follow-up |
| Winter (December–February) | Emergency furnace repair and no-heat calls | Local Services Ads and emergency repair terms kept running, spring cooling pages built a season early, spring mail and email planned |

*Example only. Adjust the months and channel mix to your climate, services and market. Season months follow Watson & Co. Marketing's April 21, 2026 guide; check-up windows follow ENERGY STAR's maintenance checklist.*

**Key Takeaway:** Plan each season's pages, emails and mail before the season starts, and shift paid budget toward emergency repair work in the summer and winter peaks.

## Where to Start With HVAC Marketing

Start with the pieces that catch urgent calls. Those are a complete Business Profile, a phone that gets answered, tracking on every call and a review request after every job. Next, add Local Services Ads and search ads split by service, then build pages for your highest-value work and plan seasonal content one season ahead. Past-customer reminders and tracked offline marketing can follow once those basics are running.
