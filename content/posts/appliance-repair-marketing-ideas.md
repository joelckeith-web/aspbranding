The appliance repair marketing ideas to start with are the ones that reach a homeowner right after a refrigerator stops cooling or a washer won't drain. That means Local Services Ads, a complete Google Business Profile, search ads split by appliance, a page for each repair you want more of, and a phone that someone answers.

This guide walks through each of those for an owner deciding where the next marketing dollar should go. It sticks to what Google, the Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) and other primary sources have published, plus how we run [appliance repair marketing](/industries/appliance-repair) at ASP. The aim is calls that come into accounts you own and booked repairs you can trace back to their source.

## How Appliance Repair Demand Works

Appliance repair is a trade of small teams. BLS counted 6,354 private appliance repair and maintenance establishments in the US in 2025, employing 24,056 people, which works out to fewer than four people per establishment on average. A separate BLS survey estimated 32,150 home appliance repairer jobs in May 2025. The two surveys measure different things, one an industry and the other an occupation, so their numbers should not be added together.

Jobs come in one broken appliance at a time. Refrigeration runs all year, and the U.S. Energy Information Administration (EIA) [lists refrigeration among the year-round home energy uses](https://www.eia.gov/energyexplained/use-of-energy/homes.php), along with water heating and lighting. That describes energy use, not repair calls, and we have not found a primary source that tracks appliance repair demand by season. Your own call and job history is the better guide to when to push harder.

We build appliance repair marketing for high call volume at lower ticket sizes. That means the marketing has to bring in steady calls and also screen out work you can't take, such as brands you don't service or addresses outside your drive time.

**Key Takeaway:** Appliance repair calls come one breakdown at a time. Plan around your own call history, and set up marketing that brings steady calls while filtering out jobs you don't want.

## Local Services Ads for Appliance Repair

Local Services Ads are Google ads that charge by the lead instead of by the click, and appliance repair qualifies. Google's [Local Services Ads overview](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/6224841?hl=en) lists "Appliance repair services" as an eligible category in the US. The ads are only offered in certain categories and areas, though, so check eligibility for yours first. You pay for leads related to your business and the services you offer. Those leads arrive as phone calls, message requests or bookings made through the ad. Google's booking integration policies list Appliance Repair among the supported home-service types in the US.

Google screens every business before its ads run. You need a public, verified Google Business Profile, and Google's [screening and verification requirements](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/12174778?hl=en) say the process takes 3–4 weeks on average after you submit your documents. Plan for that wait before you count on these leads. The verification badge on these ads is now called Google Verified.

Where your ad shows depends on more than the bid. Google's [page on Local Services Ads rankings](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/7527305?hl=en) says profile quality is a factor. It lists your rating, number of reviews, average response time, use of high-quality images and the verification checks you have completed. The same page notes that missed calls may hurt your responsiveness. For a small shop, that makes answering the phone part of the marketing, not only part of customer service.

**Key Takeaway:** Local Services Ads charge by the lead. They rank partly on your bid and partly on reviews, response time and photos, so your profile and your phone both affect what the budget buys.

## Google Business Profile and Reviews

Your Google Business Profile is the listing that shows on Google Maps and in local results. Local Services Ads also require a verified one. An appliance repair shop that works in customers' homes is a service-area business in Google's terms. Google's [Business Profile guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177?hl=en) say a service-area business should have one profile for its central office with a designated service area. They also say it can't list a "virtual" office unless that office is staffed during business hours.

Keep the profile current with the services you offer, the areas you serve, photos of your own techs and vans, regular posts and replies to reviews. Our [local SEO guide for home service businesses](/blog/local-seo-for-home-service-businesses) covers the profile in more depth. [Local SEO Pro](/local-seo-pro) is how we manage it for clients, along with citations and a monthly ranking heatmap.

Reviews do two jobs. Homeowners read them before they call, and Google counts your rating and number of reviews in Local Services Ads profile quality. Ask every customer the same way after the job, with a review link in your follow-up text or email, and reply to the reviews that come in. Google's review policies prohibit offering free or discounted goods or services in exchange for reviews. The FTC's rule on fake reviews and testimonials bans fake reviews. It also bans incentives tied to a positive or negative review. Don't set quotas for your techs or script what customers say, either.

**Key Takeaway:** Your Business Profile supports both the map and Local Services Ads, so keep it accurate and build reviews by asking every customer the same honest way.

## Search Ads and Pages by Appliance

Google Search Ads reach the homeowner who types in a specific problem, like a dishwasher that won't drain or a dryer that won't heat. We group search campaigns by appliance and service, so the ad and the page it leads to match what the person searched. A negative keyword list, which tells Google which searches should not trigger your ads, keeps spend away from work you don't take, such as brands you don't service. Our [PPC management](/ppc) page explains how we run search campaigns against booked jobs.

Your website does the same job in organic search. Decide which appliances get their own page by demand first, then by what a booked repair is worth to you, then by how strong the pages already ranking are. A repair with steady searches and good margin earns a full page. A repair nobody searches for by name can sit inside a broader page.

The details that make a repair page useful come from you or a senior tech. They include the failures you see most on each appliance, when a repair stops making sense, and what you check first on a call. We interview for those details as part of our [home service SEO](/seo) work instead of rewriting what other sites already say.

One credential is worth showing. EPA Section 608 [Type I certification](https://www.epa.gov/section608/section-608-technician-certification-requirements) covers servicing small appliances such as home refrigerators and freezers. If your techs hold it, put it on your refrigerator, freezer and ice maker pages and on your profile.

**Key Takeaway:** Split ads and pages by appliance, give a full page first to repairs with steady demand and good margin, and fill those pages with what your techs know.

## Answering Calls Fast

A homeowner with a dead refrigerator will often call the next listing when nobody picks up. That makes response speed one of the first things to fix, and our guide to [speed to lead for home service businesses](/blog/speed-to-lead-home-service-businesses) walks through the math on missed calls.

Local Services Ads give you a second reason to answer. Google counts average response time in profile quality and notes that missed calls may hurt your responsiveness. It also says message and booking leads can help, especially during nights and weekends when you can't answer the phone.

The fix is coverage for the hours your office isn't staffed. That can be a live answering service, a team member on call, or an answering system that books directly into your CRM. Either a human or an automated setup can work, as long as every call reaches a person or ends in a booking.

If you run Housecall Pro, we can set up its CSR AI to pick up missed calls and book appointments. Whatever you choose, make sure every call, answered or missed, creates a contact record, so it still gets a callback the next business day.

**Key Takeaway:** Answering speed affects both the job in front of you and how Google ranks your Local Services Ads, so cover the hours your office is closed.

## Tracking Booked Repairs and Owning Your Accounts

A call is not a booked repair, and booked repairs are what pay the bills. Tag each lead with where it came from, whether that is Local Services Ads, the map, a search ad or organic search. A working setup connects four sources. The first is call tracking for phone leads. The second is website forms with hidden UTM fields, which are tags that record which campaign a visitor came from. The third is a CRM that holds the job and its revenue, and the fourth is the ad platforms where the spend happens. Where your CRM supports it, the source follows the lead to the booked repair and its revenue. You then see cost per booked job by channel instead of a pile of calls.

Where your CRM supports it, booked revenue can also be sent back to Google Ads as an offline conversion. Bidding then follows the leads that turn into jobs instead of every lead that calls.
